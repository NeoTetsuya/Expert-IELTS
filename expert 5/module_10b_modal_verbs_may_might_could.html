May, Might & Could

Master possibility (may / might / could), formal permission (may), past ability (could), and polite requests!

May / Might

A. May & Might for Possibility & Permission

Expressing present/future chance and asking for or granting permission.

1. Possibility (Present / Future) 🔮

may / might (+ Infinitive)

Used when we think something is possible now or in the future. Both may and might are interchangeable in this sense.

  • (+) Affirmative:
    👉 "My dictionary isn't on my desk – it may / might be in my bag."
  • (-) Negative (may not / might not):
    👉 "People may not / might not have to learn English in the future."
2. Permission (Formal) 🔑

may ONLY (NOT might)

We use may to ask for, give, or refuse permission formally. Do NOT use might for permission!

  • Asking Permission (?):
    👉 "May I borrow your dictionary?"
  • Giving Permission (+):
    👉 "You may use my notes."
  • Refusing Permission (-):
    👉 "You may not take any of the books from the library."

⚡ Quick Guardrail: May vs. Might for Permission

Possibility Context
✅ Use BOTH may or might
"It may be / might be in my bag."
Permission Context
✅ Use may ONLY (Never "Might I...?")
"May I borrow...?" / "You may not take..."
Could

B. Could for Ability, Possibility & Requests

Three distinct uses of could according to Page 160.

1. Past Ability

could / couldn't

To express general ability in the past:

  • (+) Ability:
    "I could speak very good French by the time I was nine or ten."
  • (-) Inability:
    "He couldn't communicate with his grandmother until he learned German."
2. Possibility

could (Positive)

Like may/might, expresses present or future chance:

  • (+) Chance:
    "Intercultural communication could become even more important in the future."
  • ⚠️ Critical Rule for Negatives:
    In negative sentences for possibility, use may not or might not (NOT couldn't)!
3. Requests

Polite Requests

To ask someone politely to do something:

  • Could you + bare infinitive...?
    "Could you translate this for me, please?"
⚠️

Common Pitfalls & Essential Rules

Key mistakes to avoid in exam questions and formal writing.

🚫

Pitfall 1: Do NOT use "couldn't" for negative possibility!

When expressing that something is possibly NOT true, use may not or might not. Couldn't implies deduction of impossibility or past inability, NOT possibility.

❌ Incorrect: "You couldn't be able to understand her accent."
✅ Correct: "You may not / might not be able to understand her accent."
⚠️

Pitfall 2: Do NOT use "might" to ask for permission!

While may and might both work for possibility, ONLY may is used for asking or granting permission.

❌ Incorrect: "Might I borrow your dictionary?"
✅ Correct: "May I borrow your dictionary?"
