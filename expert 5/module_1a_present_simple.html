Module 1 Grammar Guide

Present Simple & Frequency

Express routines, permanent facts, habits, and daily schedules! Learn how to build positive, negative, and question forms, along with word order for frequency adverbs.

3 Main Uses of the Present Simple

When to use the base verb (or verb + -s/-es).

1

Routines & Habits

Regular, repeated actions that occur regularly.

"We study in the library most of the time."

"Doctor Meyer gives lectures every Monday and Friday."

2

Permanent Situations

States or circumstances that are ongoing and stable.

"We live in Madrid."

"My brother goes to college in York."

3

General Truths & Facts

Universal truths and scientific or general facts.

"The sun rises in the east."

"The course lasts three years."

Grammar Formulas & Sentences

Positive (+)

  • I / You / We / They speak German.
  • He / She / It speaks German. (+s / +es added!)

Negative (-)

  • I / You / We / They don't speak German.
  • He / She / It doesn't speak German. (Base verb loses -s)

Questions (?) & Short Answers

  • Do you speak German?
    ✓ Yes, I do. / ✗ No, I don't.
  • Does she speak German?
    ✓ Yes, she does. / ✗ No, she doesn't.

Adverbs & Time Expressions

Word order for expressing how frequently actions occur.

1

BEFORE Main Verbs

Adverbs sit directly between the subject and the main verb (or after don't/doesn't).

"We usually study together."

"I don't normally have lectures on Fridays."

"Do you often give presentations?"

2

AFTER Verb To Be

Adverbs sit directly after am / is / are.

"I am always tired after work."

"My tutor isn't usually late."

"Are you often busy at weekends?"

Other Time Expressions

Phrases like every day/week/month, once/twice/three times a week, or on Mondays/Fridays usually go at the beginning or end of the sentence.

Beginning of sentence "On Thursday afternoons we have a tutorial."
End of sentence "I have lunch on campus every day."

Third Person Singular Spelling (He / She / It)

How to modify base verbs in positive Present Simple sentences.

Rule 1: Most Verbs

Add -s

Simply append -s to base verb.

speak → speaks

live → lives

give → gives

Rule 2: -ch, -sh, -s, -x, -z, -o

Add -es

Add -es for ease of pronunciation.

watch → watches

go → goes

pass → passes

Rule 3: Consonant + -y

Change -y to -ies

Drop -y, then add -ies.

study → studies

carry → carries

fly → flies

Rule 4: Vowel + -y

Keep -y + -s

Do NOT drop -y if preceded by a vowel.

play → plays

enjoy → enjoys

stay → stays

💡

Important Reminder for Negative & Question Sentences

In negative sentences (doesn't) and questions (does), the main verb returns to its BASE form without -s or -es!
Say: "He doesn't live in York." (NOT "He doesn't lives").
