Modal Verb: Can & Can't

Master using can and can't to express present ability, possibility, and to ask for or grant permission!

3 Golden Rules for Modal Verb "Can"

Important features that apply to all modal verbs in English.

1

Infinitive Without "To"

After modal verbs, always use the bare infinitive (no "to").

✓ "I can speak French and Italian."

✗ "I can to speak French..."

2

No "-s" for He / She / It

Modal verbs do NOT add -s in the third person singular.

✓ "Professor Murray can help you with your assignment."

✗ "Professor Murray cans help..."

3

No "Do / Does" Auxiliary

In negative sentences and questions, the auxiliary do is NOT used.

✓ "I can't read these notes."

✓ "Can you read my essay?"

Sentence Structures & Examples

Positive (+) Subject + can + V_bare
  • I / You / We / They can speak French.
  • He / She / It can help you.
Negative (-) Subject + can't + V_bare
  • I can't read these notes.
  • She can't swim very well.

Note: can't = cannot (one word in full form).

Questions (?) & Answers Can + Subject + V_bare?
  • Can you read my essay?
    ✓ Yes, I can. / ✗ No, I can't.
  • Can we use this classroom?
    ✓ Yes, you can. / ✗ No, you can't.

3 Main Functions of Can / Can't

We use can/can't for Ability, Possibility, and Permission.

Function 1 🎸

Ability in Present

To express physical or mental skills, talents, and capabilities in the present.

"He can play the guitar and the piano."

"I can't swim very well."

Function 2 📅

Possibility

To say that something is or isn't possible given circumstances or schedule.

"You can meet your tutor tomorrow."

"I can't go out tonight – I'm too busy."

Function 3 🔑

Permission

To ask for, give, or refuse permission to do an action.

"Can we use this classroom?" (Ask)

"You can come in now." (Give)

"You can't eat in here." (Refuse)

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Keep these golden rules in mind when writing or speaking!

Pitfall #1

Adding "to" after Can

Never place "to" between can/can't and the main verb.

❌ "I can to speak French."
✅ "I can speak French."
Pitfall #2

Adding "-s" for He/She/It

Modal verbs never change form or add -s for third person.

❌ "Professor Murray cans help you."
✅ "Professor Murray can help you."
Pitfall #3

Using "Do / Does" Auxiliary

Do not use do/does/don't/doesn't with modal verbs.

❌ "Do you can read my essay?"
✅ "Can you read my essay?"
