Comparatives & Superlatives

Compare people, things, and quantities with precision! Master adjective forms, similarity structures, and noun comparisons.

Adjective Comparison Forms

How we form comparatives (-er / more) and superlatives (-est / most).

1

One-Syllable Adjectives

Add -er for comparative and the -est for superlative.

old → older → the oldest

"My sister is a year older than me."

"I am the youngest person in my family."

Spelling Special Rules:

• 1 Vowel + 1 Consonant: Double consonant (big → bigger → biggest, sad → sadder)

• Ends in -e: Add -r / -st (safe → safer → safest)

2

Two-Syllable Adjectives

Most use more and the most.

common → more common → the most common

boring → more boring → the most boring

⚠️ Exception for -y endings:

Change -y to -i + -er / -est.

heavy → heavier → heaviest

3

Long Adjectives (3+ Syllables)

Use more / the most (or less / the least).

"I'm much more confident than my brother."

"My cousin is the most intelligent person I know."

"Martin is less friendly than his sister."

"He's the least interesting teacher we have."

4

Irregular Adjectives ⭐

These common forms do not follow standard rules!

good → better → the best
bad → worse → the worst
far → further → the furthest

Comparing with (not) as ... as

Expressing equality, similarity, or difference between two items.

Similarity

(just) as + adjective + as

Shows that two people or things are equal or identical in a certain quality.

"This computer is just as expensive as that one."

Meaning: Both computers cost the exact same price.

Difference

not as + adjective + as

Means the same as "less ... than".

"I'm not as confident as I want to be."

Meaning: I have less confidence than I desire.

Interactive Price Comparison

"Laptop A is just as expensive as Laptop B."

Making Comparisons with Nouns

Use more, less, fewer, the most, the least, the fewest to compare quantities.

Noun Type Comparative Superlative Example
Uncountable Nouns
(time, money, work)		 less the least "People have less free time these days."
"My brother does the least work."
Plural Countable Nouns
(people, students, visitors) 		fewer the fewest "Fewer people live with grandparents now."
"Our town gets the fewest visitors."
Both Types
(Increases)		 more the most "There were more people at the lecture."
"Jamie does the most work."
💡

Crucial Distinction: Less vs. Fewer

Use less for things you cannot count individually (e.g., less money, less time).
Use fewer for countable items in plural form (e.g., fewer cars, fewer students).
