Adverbs & Adverbial Phrases

Master how to describe manner (how), frequency (how often), place (where), and time (when)!

A. Adverbs of Manner

Adverbs of manner tell us how we do something.

1

Regular Form: + ly

To form most adverbs of manner, add -ly to adjectives.

✓ "I learn languages quite easily." (easy → easily)

✓ "She speaks very slowly and clearly."

2

Same Form as Adjective

Some adverbs are identical to their adjective forms (e.g. fast, hard).

✓ "I can't run very fast." ✗ NOT "fastly"

✓ "My mother always works very hard."

3

Irregular Adverbs

Some adverbs do not follow standard patterns (e.g. good → well).

✓ "I usually do well in exams."

✗ "I usually do good in exams..."

⚡ Adjective to Adverb Formation Summary

Standard (+ly)
quick → quickly
clear → clearly
slow → slowly
Same Form
fast → fast
hard → hard
late → late
Irregular
good → well

B. Frequency & Time Phrases

To say how often something happens, we use two main structures.

1. Single-Word Adverbs ⏳

Adverbs of Frequency

Single words like always, usually, sometimes, rarely, never. Placed before main verbs or after be.

✓ "I rarely visit my aunt and uncle."

✓ "He is always polite and friendly."

2. Multi-Word Phrases 📅

Time Phrases

Phrases like once/twice a week, every day/month, in the morning/evening. Usually placed at the end or start.

✓ "I speak to my mum on the phone every day."

✓ "Once a week I have dinner at my grandparents' house."

C. Adverbial Phrases of Place and Time

Expressing where (place/direction) and when / how long (time).

📍

1. Adverbials of Place

Shows location, direction, or distance

Examples: here, there, abroad, past the bookshop, down the road

"My cousins live down the road from us."
"I can't study here – it's too noisy."
🕒

2. Adverbials of Time

Says when something happens or duration

Examples: today, later, at the moment, during the day, after the summer, from May to July

"My family always goes on holiday in June."
"I have a part-time job at the weekend."

💡 Typical Word Order Rule: Place + Time

When both place and time adverbials appear at the end of a sentence, we usually put PLACE first, followed by TIME.
Example: "We arrived at the station (place) at 5 o'clock (time)."
