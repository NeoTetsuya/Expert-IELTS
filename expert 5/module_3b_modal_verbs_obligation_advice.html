Have to, Must, Need to & Should

Learn how to express necessity, rules & obligations, prohibitions, and give advice!

1. Expressing Necessity & Obligation

We use have to, must, and need to when an action is necessary.

External Obligation 📋

have to

Used when the action is necessary because of external rules, laws, or someone else's authority.

✓ "I have to start work at 8.30 every morning."

(It's a company rule / employer requirement)
Internal Feeling 💡

must

Used when the speaker feels personally that the action is necessary or very important.

✓ "I must remember to book the tickets tonight."

(Personal determination / self-reminder)
General Necessity ⚡

need to

Very similar to have to. Shows that an action is necessary to achieve a requirement or goal.

✓ "I need to make some important phone calls this morning."

🔑 Quick Key Summary: Have to vs. Must

Rule / Law / Authority
👉 Use have to
"Employees have to wear safety gear."
Speaker's Personal Decision
👉 Use must
"I must call my grandma today."

2. Crucial Contrast: Mustn't vs. Don't Have to

Though positive must and have to are similar, their negatives mean completely different things!

🚫 Prohibition / Forbidden 🛑

mustn't

Means it is necessary NOT to do something or it is NOT allowed / forbidden.

"You mustn't eat a lot before exercising." (It is important that you don't – it's bad for you!)
"We mustn't use our phones in here." (It's strictly forbidden / against rules!)
🔓 No Obligation / Optional 🎉

don't have to / don't need to

Means something is NOT necessary. You can do it if you want, but you don't need to.

"I don't have to wear a uniform at work." (It's not necessary – I wear whatever I like!)
"My brother doesn't have to get up early today." (Because he starts work at 4 p.m.)
"He doesn't need to complete the report now." (He can do it tomorrow instead.)
⚠️

Common English Learner Trap!

Never confuse mustn't and don't have to!
• "You mustn't come." = Do NOT come! You are forbidden.
• "You don't have to come." = You are free to choose! Come if you want, but no pressure.

3. Giving Advice: Should & Shouldn't

We use should and shouldn't to express whether an action is or isn't a good idea.

Good Idea (+) 👍

should + bare infinitive

Used to give positive advice or recommend a wise course of action.

✓ "You should check your spelling before you print a document."

(Advice: it is a good idea to double check!)
Bad Idea (-) 👎

shouldn't + bare infinitive

Used to advise someone against doing something because it's a bad idea.

✓ "You shouldn't speak to your boss like that."

(Advice: speaking like that is unwise/rude)

💡 Advice vs. Obligation Difference

• Should is softer than must/have to. If you don't do what you "should" do, it's just a bad idea or poor choice—not a crime or rule violation.
• Must / Have to carry force, rules, or strict necessity!
