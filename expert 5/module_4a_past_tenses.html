Module 4 Grammar Guide

Past Simple & Continuous

Master the past! Learn how to describe finished events, actions in progress, and what happens when one action interrupts another.

The Past Simple

For actions, states, or situations that started and finished in the past.

Common Time Expressions

yesterday two days/months/years ago last week/month/year in 2016

"I joined the course two weeks ago."

"Last year I was very ill."

How to form it

Positive (+)

Add -ed for regular verbs.

"We finished work early."

*Irregular verbs must be memorized (e.g., write → wrote).

Negative (-)

Use didn't + base verb.

"I didn't walk to work."

Questions (?)

Use did + subject + base verb.

"Did you do any exercise?"

Spelling Rules for Regular Verbs (+ed)

Most Verbs

Add -ed

start → started

work → worked

Ends in -e or -ee

Add -d

live → lived

agree → agreed

Consonant + y

Change y to i, add -ed

study → studied

carry → carried

1 Vowel + Consonant

Double consonant, add -ed

stop → stopped

plan → planned

The Past Continuous

For actions that were in progress at a particular time in the past.

1

Action in Progress

Happening at a specific, stated time.

"We were working at 10 o'clock this morning."

2

Two Actions

In progress at the same time (often with while).

"I was studying while my friends were enjoying themselves."

3

Background Scene

Used at the start of a story to set the scene.

"It was raining and it was getting colder and colder."

How to form it: was/were + -ing form

Positive (+)

  • I/He/She/It was working.
  • You/We/They were working.

Negative (-)

  • I/He/She/It wasn't working.
  • You/We/They weren't working.

Questions (?)

  • Was I/he/she/it working?
  • Were you/we/they working?

The Interrupted Action

We often use the past continuous with the past simple together to talk about an action that happened while another longer action was in progress.

Long Action (Past Continuous)
Short Action (Past Simple)

"I was waiting for the bus when it began to rain."

Using 'When' and 'While'

"I was running in the park when I met Ana."

Followed by Past Simple

We use when before the short, interrupting action (Past Simple).

"While I was swimming, somebody stole my purse."

Followed by Past Continuous

We use while before the longer action that was in progress (Past Continuous).

⚠️ Sequential Actions

When one action happens after another in a sequence, we use the Past Simple for both.

"I fell over and hurt my leg."

🚫 Stative Verbs

We use the Past Simple, not the Past Continuous, with stative verbs (understand, know, believe, etc.).

"I didn't understand what he was saying."
