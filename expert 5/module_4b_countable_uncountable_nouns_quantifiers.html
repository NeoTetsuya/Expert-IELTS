Master the differences between countable and uncountable nouns, and learn how to pair them accurately with quantifiers!

A. Countable vs. Uncountable Nouns

Understanding singular/plural verb agreement and noun behavior.

1. Countable Nouns 🔢

Can be counted (1, 2, 3...)

Countable nouns have both singular and plural forms (e.g., country → countries, animal → animals, plant → plants).

  • • Singular noun + Singular verb: "This plant is very common in my region."
  • • Plural noun + Plural verb: "There are a number of animals that are endangered."
Examples:

beach, river, mountain, animal, park, country, city, tiger, document

2. Uncountable Nouns 🌊

Cannot be counted

Uncountable nouns do NOT have a plural form. They are ALWAYS followed by a singular verb.

  • • Always Singular Verb: "The doctor's advice was very helpful."
  • • No "s" ending: We never say advices, informations, or traffics.
Key Uncountable Examples (Page 64):

information, advice, traffic, money, research, rain, luggage, time, wildlife

B. Quantifiers Breakdown

Which quantifiers match which noun type? (Page 64 Rules)

Both Types 🤝

some / any / a lot of / enough

Use with plural countable AND uncountable nouns.

  • (+) Positive: "We need some more rain." / "some birds"
  • (-) Negative: "Haven't got any high mountains." / "don't need any more research."
  • (?) Question: "Are there any rivers?" / "got any information?"
Plural Countable Only 🌴

many / a few / too many

Used exclusively with plural countable nouns.

  • a few = small number: "There are a few good beaches near here."
  • too many = excess: "There are too many animals in that zoo."
  • (how) many: "How many animals...?" / "aren't many tigers"
Uncountable Only ⏳

much / a little / too much

Used exclusively with uncountable nouns.

  • a little = small amount: "I've only got a little time to see the country."
  • too much = excess: "I take too much luggage when I travel."
  • (how) much: "How much money...?" / "haven't got much time"
