Module 5 Grammar Guide

-ing Forms vs. Infinitives

Unlock the rules of verb patterns. Learn exactly when to use an "-ing" form, when to use "to + verb", and when to use the base verb alone.

The -ing Form (Gerunds)

When a verb acts like a noun, we add -ing to it.

1

As a Subject

Used at the beginning of a sentence, functioning exactly like a noun.

"Travelling by plane is very expensive."

"I think visiting the country is a good way to learn."

2

After Prepositions

If a verb immediately follows a preposition (in, on, at, before, after, etc.), it MUST be -ing.

"We took photos before going home."

"After seeing the sights, we went back."

3

After Certain Verbs

Some verbs naturally require the next verb to end in -ing. (See list below).

"I really don't like flying."

"I avoid going there in summer."

Common Verbs followed by -ing:

avoid begin (can't) stand consider continue enjoy finish hate imagine keep like love miss prefer start stop suggest

Infinitives with 'To'

Formed with: to + base verb

1

To Express Purpose

Answers the question "Why?". It explains the reason for doing something.

"I went to London to go shopping."

"I'd like to go to Russia to visit my uncle."

2

After Adjectives

Used after adjectives describing feelings or opinions (surprised, difficult, nice).

"I was surprised to find it so modern."

"It is difficult to find cheap accommodation."

3

After Certain Verbs

Some verbs naturally require a to-infinitive directly after them.

"They advised us to travel by coach."

"I can't afford to go on holiday."

Common Verbs followed by 'to' + verb:

advise agree allow arrange ask can't afford can't wait decide expect forget help hope learn offer plan promise refuse want would like

Infinitives Without 'To'

Also known as the Bare Infinitive (just the base verb).

1. After Modal Verbs

can, could, may, might, must, should, will, would.

"You should definitely go to the mountains."

"You can't get there by train."

2. After 'Make' and 'Let'

These verbs follow a very specific formula. Do not use 'to'!

make/let + person/object + bare verb

"You can't make him apologise."

"My parents didn't let me travel alone."
