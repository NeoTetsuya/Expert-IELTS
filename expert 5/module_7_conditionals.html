Module 7 Grammar Guide

Conditionals (Zero & First)

Master cause and effect. Learn how to construct sentences about general truths and likely future results using "If" and "Unless".

General Truths & Facts

Used for things that are always or generally true.

The Formula

If / When + Present Simple , Present Simple

"If you live in the city centre, you probably live in a flat or apartment."

"I always stop and talk to my neighbours when I see them."

The Comma Rule

The if clause can come at the beginning or after the main clause. Watch how the comma changes!

If I'm home alone
,
I always lock all the doors.

Rule: When "If" is at the beginning, we MUST use a comma.

Unless = If Not

We can use unless in the if clause to replace a negative "if".

Using If Not

"A town isn't attractive if it doesn't have good facilities."

Using Unless

"A town isn't attractive unless it has good facilities."

Likely Future Results

Used for something that may happen in the future as a result of an action.

The Formula

If + Present Simple , Will + Infinitive

"If they open a community centre, it 'll be very popular."

"If they build a new shopping centre, it will be good for the local community."

The Comma Rule (Same as Zero!)

If crime continues to rise
,
people won't want to go out at night.

Unless applied to the Future

You can use unless in the First Conditional too.

"The sports centre will have to close if they don't raise prices."

"The sports centre will have to close unless they raise prices."
