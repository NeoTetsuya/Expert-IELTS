Module 8 Grammar Guide

The Present Perfect

Connect the past to the present. Learn how to talk about life experiences, ongoing states, and recent actions with powerful time phrases.

4 Main Uses

The Present Perfect always has a strong connection to now.

1

Indefinite Past (Present Result)

Happened at an unknown time in the past, but has a visible result now.

"My sister has hurt her arm playing tennis."

Result: Her arm is injured right now.

2

Continuing States/Actions

Started in the past and continues into the present. We use for or since.

"I haven't played football for six months."

"I have been captain since last year."

3

Life Experiences

Experiences in someone's life up to now. We often use ever and never.

"He has taken part in national competitions."

"Have you ever played hockey?"

4

Unfinished Time Periods

The time period (today, this week, this year) hasn't finished yet.

"I have run ten kilometers this morning."

Note: It is still morning when this is spoken.

Building the Present Perfect

SUBJECT + HAVE / HAS + PAST PARTICIPLE

Positive

  • I/You/We/They have eaten
  • He/She/It has eaten

Negative

  • I/You/We/They haven't eaten
  • He/She/It hasn't eaten

Questions

  • Have I/you/we/they eaten?
  • Has he/she/it eaten?
📝
Past Participle Note

For regular verbs, add -ed (work → worked, study → studied).
Irregular verbs must be memorized! (drive → driven, teach → taught, see → seen).

We frequently use specific time words to add meaning to the Present Perfect.

Just

Recent

Means a very short time ago. It goes right before the past participle.

"My team has just scored a goal."

Already

Earlier

Means earlier than expected. Usually goes before the past participle.

"I've already bought tickets for the match."

Yet

Expected

Used in negatives and questions. Usually goes at the very end.

"Have you heard the results yet?"

Other Common Time Phrases

ever never before recently lately so far

"I haven't had time to do any exercise lately."

"We've won six matches so far this season."
