A. Each, Every & Two-Item Quantifiers

Distinguishing individual members, groups, and dual pairs.

1. Each vs. Every 🔍

Singular Noun + Singular Verb

Both take a singular noun and singular verb.

  • Each: Focuses on thinking about each person or thing separately.
    👉 "I read each article in the magazine very carefully."
  • Every: Focuses on thinking about all members together as a group.
    👉 "Every TV programme seems to be the same."
2. Both / Either / Neither ✌️

Used for Exactly 2 Things

Refers to two people or things.

  • Both (of) + Plural Noun + Plural Verb: (1 AND the other)
    👉 "Both of these articles are useful for your assignment."
  • Either (of) + Singular Noun + Singular Verb: (1 OR the other)
    👉 "Either of these laptops is suitable for you."
  • Neither (of) + Singular Noun + Singular Verb: (NOT 1 or the other)
    👉 "Unfortunately, neither of these tablets has the right software."
Several

several + Plural Noun + Plural Verb

Means 'more than a few but not a lot':

"Several friends of mine are studying journalism."
Plenty Of

plenty of + Plural / Uncountable Noun

Means 'a large number or amount' (more than enough):

📚 Plural: "There are plenty of books to help with research."
💰 Uncountable: "You need plenty of money to set up a business."

B. Group Quantifiers & Amount Phrases

Quantifying specific groups, zero amounts, and precise quantity expressions.

All / Most / Some

Specific Groups

Used with (of) before specific nouns:

  • + Plural Noun + Plural Verb:
    "All the electronic devices I own are very old."
  • + Uncountable Noun + Singular Verb:
    "Most news these days is very depressing."
  • "I think some of the information on this website is wrong."
No vs. None Of

Negative Meaning (+) Verb

Both have negative meanings; use with affirmative verbs!

  • No + Plural/Uncountable:
    "There are no messaging apps on my phone." / "There is no ink."
  • None of + Uncountable (Singular V):
    "None of the information was very helpful."
  • None of + Plural (Singular OR Plural V):
    "None of these news articles is / are very interesting."
Specific Phrases

Amount / Number Phrases

Formal & natural phrases for quantity:

  • a great deal of + Uncountable:
    "don't have a great deal of money"
  • amount of + Uncountable:
    "a small amount of time"
  • number of + Plural:
    "a large number of useful apps"
  • a couple of + Plural:
    "a couple of good TV programmes"

⚡ Amount vs. Number Quick Guardrail

amount of money / time / info

C. Indefinite Pronouns Table & Rules

Combining some-, any-, no-, every- with -one, -body, -thing.

Prefix People (-one / -body) Things (-thing) Typical Usage Context
some- someone / somebody something Positive (+): "I know someone who works in film."
any- anyone / anybody anything Negatives (-) & Questions (?): "don't know anyone" / "Is there anything?"
Or (+) for "any person/thing without preference": "Anyone can take photos."
no- no one / nobody nothing Negative meaning with (+) Affirmative verb: "Nobody seems to know." / "produced nothing."
every- everyone / everybody everything All members (takes SINGULAR verb): "Everyone in my family uses instant messaging."
⚠️

Crucial Pronoun Reference Agreement Rule!

ALL indefinite pronouns take a SINGULAR VERB (e.g. Everyone is / Nobody knows). However, when referring back to them later in the sentence, use plural pronouns: they, them, their, or themselves!

👉 "Everyone should carry a mobile so they can use it in an emergency."
