Task IELTS Writing Task 1 Prompt

The charts below give information about water availability and use in the agricultural industry.

Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Write at least 150 words.

Model answer & Method Annotations

174 words • Model Answer calibrated for Band 6 Target
Chart Synthesis & Overview

Look for the connections between the charts. State what both charts illustrate, followed by an overall trend.

💡 "The pie chart and bar chart both illustrate water use. We can see from the chart that certain types of farming use less water than others."
Connectors & Relations

Use connectors to organise ideas and show relations.

⭐ "However, only one percent...", "Generally, vegetable crops...", "Rice, however, uses about three times more...".
Grammar: -ing Forms as Nouns

Use -ing forms as nouns (gerunds) to create concise grammatical structures.

💡 "certain types of farming use less water", "types of farming make more demands".
Paragraph 1: Introduction & General Overview

The pie chart and bar chart both illustrate water use. We can see from the chart that certain types of farming use less water than others.

Paragraph 2: Pie Chart Features (Usable Water & Groundwater Proportion)

The pie chart shows that only one percent of all the water on the planet can be used for human purposes, and the vast majority of that is in the form of groundwater. However, only one percent of that usable water can be found in rivers or lakes.

Paragraph 3: Bar Chart Features (Crops, Multiples & Beef Extreme)

The bar chart shows that certain types of farming make more demands on the amount of water used than others. Generally, vegetable crops require lower amounts of water, around 1,000-2,000 litres of water for every kilo of food produced. Rice, however, uses about three times more, at around 3,000 litres for every kilo. The biggest difference in water consumption is in meat products. A kilo of beef needs ten times more water than a kilo of chicken, at 4,000 litres and 42,000 litres respectively.

Paragraph 4: Final Summary / Synthesis

So the charts show that food choices may impact water management.

Paragraph Progression & Units

Use paragraphs to show a clear overall progression.

What are you measuring in the second chart? Ensure you state the exact unit: litres of water for every kilo of food produced.

⭐ Para 1: Intro/Overview • Para 2: Pie chart • Para 3: Bar chart • Para 4: Concluding synthesis.
Anaphoric Reference

Use words like that to refer back to ideas without cumbersome noun repetitions.

💡 "...only one percent of all the water... and the vast majority of that is in the form of groundwater."
Comparative Multipliers

Use comparative forms to make relationships between ideas clear.

⭐ "three times more", "ten times more water than a kilo of chicken", "at 4,000 litres and 42,000 litres respectively".

Band 6 Task 1 Formula

A kilo of beef needs ten times more water than...

Why this achieves Band 6:

Demonstrates numerical comparison using multiplier structures ("ten times more than") and cohesive adverbial linkers ("respectively") instead of listing isolated figures.

Band 6 Standard Pattern Example:

"A kilo of beef needs ten times more water than a kilo of chicken, at 4,000 litres and 42,000 litres respectively."

Band 6 Dual-Chart Toolkit (Module 3)

1. Dual-Chart Report Skeleton (Pie & Bar Comparison)

Introduction & Overview:

The pie chart and bar chart both illustrate [combined topic]. We can see from the chart that [general trend: certain types use less water than others].

Body 1 (Pie Chart - Availability & Breakdown):

The pie chart shows that only [small proportion] of all the [resource] can be used for [purpose], and the vast majority of that is in the form of [Category A]. However, only [small proportion] of that usable water can be found in [Category B].

Body 2 (Bar Chart - Demand, Multipliers & Extremes):

The bar chart shows that certain types of farming make more demands on [resource] than others. Generally, [Group A] require lower amounts, around [X] litres for every [unit]. [Item B], however, uses about [multiplier] more. The biggest difference is in [Group C], where [Item D] needs [multiplier] more than [Item E], at [X] and [Y] respectively.

Final Synthesis:

So the charts show that [consumer/production choices] may impact [resource management].
