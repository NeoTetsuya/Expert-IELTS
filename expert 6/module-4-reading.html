Which weird science stories have proved a hit on social media?

Unusual science stories in the media soon get noticed. They then get rapidly passed on via social networking sites. The stories that follow were some of the science news items that have recently had us all thinking: ‘Oh my goodness, really?’ They come from a published list of the scientific articles that proved to be the most popular on social media, as indicated by the number of likes, shares and mentions that each of these received.

One of the top stories related to artificial sweeteners, which many people add to their tea or coffee in an attempt to lose weight by reducing their sugar intake. However, it now appears that this may actually be more harmful than using sugar itself. Experiments on mice and humans suggest that common sugar alternatives change the metabolism, causing blood sugar levels to rise and causing diseases by changing the beneficial bacteria in the human body. The trial on humans who were given the artificial sweetener saccharin suggested such changes to the body could begin to occur after just four days. Scientists involved in the study said the widespread use of the chemicals in diet drinks and food could be contributing to a range of health problems.

A rather less serious scientific study relating to sweets has also recently proved popular on social media sites. In this, researchers were attempting to find out how long it would take for doctors and other medical professionals to finish a box of chocolates when it was left in a hospital ward environment. Two 350g boxes of chocolates were placed in four wards in three different hospitals where they were watched in secret with the time recorded when each chocolate was eaten. The researchers concluded that ‘chocolate survival in a hospital ward was relatively short, with a median survival time per chocolate of 51 minutes’. It was discovered that chocolates were consumed first by healthcare assistants and nurses, followed by doctors. However, the conclusion was that further practical studies were required.

Another scientific study focusing on how long something is likely to last has also been widely shared on social networks. This discussed the popularity of those very sites. Will the popularity of, for example, Facebook, Instagram and Twitter last forever? It certainly will not, according to the Princeton University researchers, who, after conducting an investigation with the aim of answering this question, concluded: that ‘Ideas, like diseases, tend to spread infectiously between people before eventually dying out. In this they have been successfully compared with medical epidemics.’ According to the study, the growth of a social media site resembles an infectious disease that quickly dies after a quick rise. A site that is as its peak today, then, is likely to lose 80 percent of its users in the next three years.

The idea of time travel - being able to move forward into the future or backwards into a previous age – is something that is usually a concern of science fiction rather than science. However, a team of scientists from the physics department of Michigan Technological University have been searching for evidence of time travellers from the future visiting the world today. Their approach is to search online for references to people or events posted before the person in question had been born or the event had happened. For example, they used Google and other search engines to try to find mentions of Comet ISON before its discovery in 2012. So far they have not found evidence of any time travellers, but they stress that their failure to do so may just be because such travellers ‘may not want to be found and may be extremely good at covering their tracks’.

A further scientific study enjoying huge recent social network success relates to human communication across geographical distance rather than across time. For the first time, scientists have been able to send a simple mental message from one person to another without any contact between the two, despite the fact that they were thousands of miles apart. This research was led by experts at Harvard University and it used technology to enable brain-to-brain communication between a subject in India and another one in France.

It is easy to understand why, for different reasons, science stories like the five outlined above caught the interest and the imagination of millions. But what will the next story be that will start in an academic journal and end up going viral?
