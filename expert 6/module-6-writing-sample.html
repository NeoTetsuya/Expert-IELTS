Task IELTS Writing Task 1 Prompt

The graph and diagram below give information on changes to road design in a U.S. city in 2010.

Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Write at least 150 words.

🚲
Infrastructure Redesign: 2 wider lanes converted into 4 narrower lanes + 2 protected cycle tracks with a concrete barrier. Heavy trucks are restricted from the curb-adjacent lane.
📈
Commuter Growth: Bicycle-friendly cities saw frequent bike commuting surge from 0.8% to 1.4%, far outpacing the modest US national average rise from ~0.4% to ~0.5%.

Model answer & Method Annotations

178 words • Model Answer calibrated for Band 6 Target
Introduction Strategy

Start by saying what the diagram and graph show but paraphrase the test task.

💡 "The diagram and line graph give an overview of changes to approaches to travelling by bicycle."
Trend & Stability Language

Use verbs and adverbs to describe change or stability.

⭐ "remained quite stable", "increased slightly", "rose from 0.8% to 1.4%".
Grammar: Tense Control

Use the correct tenses. The diagram looks at the present and the future ("will mainly involve", "will not be allowed"). The graph looks at the past ("remained", "chose", "rose").

Paragraph 1: Introduction & Overall Purpose Overview

The diagram and line graph give an overview of changes to approaches to travelling by bicycle. The diagram illustrates plans to separate large vehicles and cyclists, to make the roads more attractive for cyclists.

Paragraph 2: Line Graph Features (Past Trends: US Average vs. Bike-Friendly Cities)

The line graph shows that, overall, the number of people cycling to work in the USA remained quite stable from 2000 to 2005, but increased slightly by 2010, when 0.5% of people chose to cycle regularly to work. However, in cities with a bicycle-friendly policy, the percentage of people who chose bicycle transport rose from 0.8% to 1.4% by the end of the period.

Paragraph 3: Diagram Features (Lanes, Barrier & Vehicle Restrictions)

The planned changes to the bridge approach road mainly involve increasing the number of traffic lanes from two to four. Larger vehicles will not be allowed to travel in the lane nearest to the cyclists. By decreasing the size of the lanes, they will be able to make space to add two new cycle lanes, with a barrier to separate cyclists and other motor traffic. This should make the road more attractive for both cyclists and people travelling by foot.

Overview & Relevance

Give an overview of the main features.

Do NOT include irrelevant information: for example, decorative drawings like the man and tree are not important. Focus on lanes, barrier, and vehicle restrictions.

💡 "The diagram illustrates plans to separate large vehicles and cyclists, to make the roads more attractive for cyclists."
Data & Figures Precision

Use data and numbers to support your ideas. Quote exact key percentages (0.5%, 0.8%, 1.4%) and lane counts (two to four).

⭐ "when 0.5% of people chose to cycle regularly to work", "rose from 0.8% to 1.4% by the end of the period."
Punctuation & Linking

Use correct punctuation to construct accurate compound and complex sentences (e.g. commas after introductory clauses like "By decreasing the size of the lanes," and before "with a barrier to separate...").

1. Mixed Graph & Diagram Report Skeleton

Introduction & Dual Overview:

The diagram and line graph give an overview of [topic]. The diagram illustrates plans to [key planned change in infrastructure] to [intended outcome].

Body 1 (Line Graph - Past Trends & Comparisons):

The line graph shows that, overall, [Variable A: national average] remained quite stable from [Year] to [Year], but increased slightly by [Year], when [X]% of people [action]. However, in [Variable B: policy cities], the percentage rose from [X]% to [Y]% by the end of the period.

Body 2 (Diagram - Road Layout & Planned Restructuring):

The planned changes to [infrastructure] mainly involve [increasing/decreasing lanes from X to Y]. Larger vehicles will not be allowed to [restriction]. By [decreasing lane sizes], they will be able to make space to [add cycle tracks and barrier]. This should make the road more attractive for both [User Group 1] and [User Group 2].
