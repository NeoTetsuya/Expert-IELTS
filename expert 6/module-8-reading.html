Food Processors and Diets

A Every few years another method of dieting becomes popular. Many centre on one or other specific foods supposed to have unusual powers. Widely practised from the 1930s onwards, the grapefruit diet encouraged people to avoid carbohydrates and to eat foods rich in fat and protein together with a grapefruit. The idea was that grapefruits have a miraculous ability to burn fat, so eating the two at the same time would result in the bikini body you always dreamed of. Another rather unappetising diet that has been popular for the last sixty years is the cabbage soup diet. In this, dieters have attempted to achieve rapid weight loss by eating nothing but cabbage soup for seven days. Other regimes permit the dieter to eat any raw fruit and vegetables they like as long as they liquidise them into a drinkable smoothie.

B Research has found that people who persevere with a diet in the long term often attribute their success to the purchase of a food processor. This device makes it quick and easy for the user to produce soups or smoothies, thus simplifying the preparation of foods that fit the particular rules the dieter is trying to observe.

C So how does this invaluable piece of kitchen equipment work? It consists of a motor which is located in the base of the machine. This is the heaviest part of the food processor. In the kind of machine used in a professional kitchen it can be as heavy as nine kilos. Then there is a bowl — that's the part where you put the food you want to process. How big this is will vary depending on the size of the processor. The capacity can range from two cups in the smallest bowl to thirteen in the largest size. The bowl has a lid to prevent the food from splashing out when the machine is in use. Somewhere in the middle range will probably be right for most dieters. Although if you opt for a bigger bowl, you can take advantage of it by making more and freezing any leftovers.

D You put the food into the bowl via a tube together with a plunger which presses the food down. The plunger helps protect your fingers from coming into contact with the machine's sharp blades. It also means you do not have to touch the food any more than is essential. This makes the process dramatically different from another way of attempting to lose weight that became popular in the nineteenth century. This was known as Fletcherism after the man who devised the practice. He was an American called Horace Fletcher who unflaggingly promoted the idea of chewing each mouthful of food at least thirty-two times, or until it was in liquid form. Although food processors provide a speedier way of liquidising food, Fletcher believed that it was the chewing process that helped people to lose weight and avoid disease. It also made him a fortune, as it was taken up by a great many people including the business tycoon, John D. Rockefeller and the world-famous writer Franz Kafka.

E The food processor comes with a number of attachments which you fix to a shaft in the centre of the bowl. One of the most useful of these is known as the Sabatier. This is an S-shaped blade, and its name is used to describe kitchen knives made of top-quality steel. Other useful attachments are the shredding and slicing discs which will allow you not only to cut up fruit and vegetables for smoothies and salads but also to prepare more advanced dishes such as curry sauces, with the result that the temptation to give up on your diet is reduced.

F Of course, for some diets a food processor is superfluous. One of the earliest bestselling diet books was called The Art of Living Long. It was written in 1558 by the Venetian Luigi Cornaro. From his late thirties, Cornaro had restricted himself to 400 grams of food a day, although he additionally allowed himself 500 grams of wine. Later in his life he even limited his daily eating to just one egg. Surprisingly perhaps, he survived until the age of 98. Cornaro would clearly have had no need for a food processor. But making salads and smoothies the basis of a diet is a much more sensible approach for the rest of us. So get that processor plugged in.
