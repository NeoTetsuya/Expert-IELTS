Geological Diagram: Stages of Waterfall Formation

3 Chronological Stages
Stage 1: Initial Flow Horizontal
Water flows horizontally over a resilient layer of hard rock situated over softer underlying rock.
Stage 2: Differential Erosion Slope Forms
Soft rock erodes at a faster rate, developing a distinct cliff face and causing water to plunge downward.
Stage 3: Overhang Collapse & Gorge Waterfall & Plunge Pool
Unsupported hard rock collapses into plunge pool; swirling fallen debris deepens the pool and retreats upstream to form a gorge.

Sequencers & Stage Linkers

Use sequencers where appropriate to organise your ideas clearly through time.

⭐ "At first...", "Over time...", "This continues until...", "eventually", "In the final stage..."
Paragraph 1: Introduction & Overall Process Principle

The diagram shows the process of waterfall formation over a long period of time. The waterfall is formed when water flows over hard rock which sits over soft rock.

Paragraph 2: Body 1 (Horizontal Flow to Overhang Collapse)

At first, the water flows across the rock horizontally. Over time, the soft rock erodes and a cliff is formed which causes the water to move downhill. This continues until the erosion creates a much steeper cliff. The hard rock at the end of the cliff eventually falls because there is nothing to hold it and it drops into the pool below. The water no longer flows across the rock. Instead it drops over the cliff and falls straight down into the pool. The remaining water flows out of the pool and the river continues downstream.

Paragraph 3: Body 2 (Plunge Pool Deepening & Upstream Gorge Formation)

The rocks that fell from the edge of the cliff into the pool move around with the force of the water. This results in more erosion and causes the pool to become bigger. In the final stage of the process, a gorge is formed.

Overall Process Summary

Give an overview of the diagram. What is the main principle you learn from the stages?

💡 "The waterfall is formed when water flows over hard rock which sits over soft rock."
Dynamic Process Verbs

Passive Voice Accuracy

Use the passive form where appropriate to describe what is formed, shaped, or created.

Band 6 Process Toolkit (Module 8)

1. Natural Process Essay Skeleton

Introduction & Core Overview:

The diagram shows the process of [process noun phrase] over [timescale]. [Phenomenon] is formed when [material A] acts on [material B], causing [fundamental change].

Body 1 (Initial Flow & Early Erosion):

At first, [element] flows across [surface] [direction]. Over time, [weaker layer] erodes and [new feature] is formed, which causes [movement]. This continues until [erosion effect] creates [steeper formation]. The [top layer] eventually falls because [reason] and drops into [receptacle].

Body 2 (Deepening, Expansion & Final Stage):

The [debris/rocks] that fell into [pool] move around with [force of nature]. This results in [secondary erosion] and causes [pool/channel] to become [larger/deeper]. In the final stage of the process, [terminal structure] is formed.

2. Process Sequencers & Geological Collocations

  • waterfall formation geological development
  • hard rock / soft rock resilient vs. softer stratum
  • erodes / erosion wears away / abrasion
  • steep cliff / overhang vertical rock face
  • plunge pool pool at waterfall base
  • gorge is formed steep canyon created upstream
  • force of the water hydraulic power / swirling
