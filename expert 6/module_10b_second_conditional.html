Second Conditional

Explore hypothetical possibilities! Learn how to describe unreal, unlikely, or imaginary situations using If + Past Simple, Would + Infinitive.

Unreal, Unlikely or Imaginary Situations

We use the second conditional to talk about hypothetical scenarios and their imagined results in the present or future.

Second Conditional Formula

If + Past Simple, Would + Infinitive

Although we use the Past Simple tense in the if clause, we are talking about present or future imaginary situations (NOT the past!).

If Clause (Condition)

If + Subject + Past Simple

e.g., If I lived abroad... / If I were younger...

Main Clause (Result)

Subject + would / 'd + Base Verb

e.g., ...I would try to learn the language. / ...I'd get involved.

3 Types of Hypothetical Contexts

Scenario 1 🌍

Unlikely Situation

Possible in theory, but unlikely to happen in reality.

"If I lived abroad, I would try hard to learn the local language."

(Reality: I do not live abroad and have no plans to.)

Scenario 2 ⌛

Unreal / Contrary to Fact

Completely impossible or contrary to current present facts.

"If I were younger, I'd get more involved in my community."

(Reality: I am older; I cannot change my age.)

Scenario 3 ✨

Imagined Improvement

Imagining how things would be different if behavior changed.

"If everyone took more care over litter, the town would look much better."

(Reality: People currently drop litter.)

Was vs. Were in the First Person (If I was / If I were)

Both forms are used in English, but they differ in level of formal academic appropriateness.

Academic Written English 🎓

"If I WERE..."

Standard, formal register. Highly recommended for university essays, academic writing, and exams.

"If I were setting up a business here, I’d insist on a faster internet connection."

"If I were younger, I’d get more involved."

Spoken / Informal English 🗣️

"If I WAS..."

Common in everyday casual conversation and informal writing, though frowned upon in strict academic contexts.

"If I was able to learn another language, I’d learn Arabic."

"If I was you, I'd ask for help."

Clause Order & Comma Rules

You can place either the if clause or the main clause first without altering the sentence meaning!

Order 1: If Clause First ⚠️

Requires a Comma ( , )

When the sentence begins with the If clause, place a comma between the two clauses.

"If everyone followed the rules, life would be much easier."

Order 2: Main Clause First ✔️

NO Comma Needed

When the sentence begins with the Main clause, do NOT use a comma before if.

"Life would be much easier if everyone followed the rules."
