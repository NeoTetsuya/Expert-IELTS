Present Simple

A. Present Simple Core Uses & Rules

Used for regular routines, habits, general truths, and permanent situations.

Use 1 🔄

Regular & Repeated Actions

Describes habits, repeated routines, scheduled timetables, and recurring frequency.

👉 "Most people pay a monthly fee for their mobile."

👉 "The computer system updates every Monday morning."

👉 "The office doesn't open on Wednesday afternoons."

Use 2 🏢

Permanent States & Scientific Facts

Describes long-term states, permanent conditions, job positions, or universal facts.

👉 "This telecom company offers free email accounts."

👉 "My family lives near the coast."

👉 "Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius."

Forming Present Simple Sentences

Use the base form of the verb. For he / she / it, add -s / -es.

Positive (+)

I / You / We / They like tech apps.

He / She / It likes tech apps. (+s)

Negative (-)

I / You / We / They don't like social media.

He / She / It doesn't like social media.

Questions (?)

Do you use this software?
✓ Yes, I do. / ✗ No, I don't.

Does she use this software?
✓ Yes, she does. / ✗ No, she doesn't.

Present Continuous

B. Present Continuous Uses & Formulas

Formed with be (am/is/are) + verb-ing for current, evolving, or temporary actions.

Use 1 ⚡

Happening Right Now

To describe activities taking place at the exact time of speaking or writing.

👉 "The IT manager is updating the website right now."
Use 2 ⏳

Temporary Situations & Trends

Actions happening around now for a limited time, or ongoing changing trends.

👉 "Dr Brown's working on a new project this summer."

👉 "Smartphone prices are decreasing rapidly."

⚡ Present Continuous Structure Formulas

Positive (+)

Subject + am / is / are + v-ing

"They're working on the system."

Negative (-)

Subject + am not / isn't / aren't + v-ing

"He isn't living on campus this term."

Questions (?) Inversion

Am / Is / Are + subject + v-ing?

"Are you studying for the module test?"

C. State Verbs & Dual-Meaning Verbs

State verbs describe states/conditions, not actions. They are not normally used in a continuous tense unless their meaning changes!

Group 1

Emotions & Thoughts

Express mental states, opinions, preferences, or promises:

agree • disagree • hate • like • mind • need • prefer • promise • seem • understand • want
Group 2

The 5 Senses

Express non-deliberate sensory perceptions:

feel • hear • see • smell • sound • taste
Group 3

Owning & Possession

Express ownership, measures, or relationships:

belong to • have • own • weigh • cost
Crucial Grammar Rule

🔄 Dual-Meaning Verbs Interactive Switcher

Some verbs can be BOTH state verbs and dynamic verbs depending on their meaning!

⚠️

State Verb Common Pitfall Guardrail

❌ Incorrect: "I am wanting a new smartphone right now."
✅ Correct: "I want a new smartphone right now." (Want is purely a state verb expressing a desire, so use Present Simple even for 'right now'!)

🔁 Present Simple
  • Focus: Regular routines, habits, timetables, and long-term permanent states.
  • Signal Words: always, usually, often, sometimes, never, every week, on Mondays.
  • Example: "I work at a tech company." (Permanent job)
⏳ Present Continuous
  • Focus: Actions in progress right now, temporary situations, evolving trends.
  • Signal Words: now, right now, at the moment, currently, this term, this week.
  • Example: "I am working from home this week." (Temporary setup)
