Present Tenses & State Verbs

Master Present Simple for habits & permanent facts, Present Continuous for actions happening now & temporary states, and identify non-continuous State Verbs!

Present Simple

A. Present Simple Uses & Sentence Form

Used for regular routines, habits, and permanent situations.

Use 1 🔄

Regular & Repeated Actions

We use the Present Simple to talk about habits, repeated routines, and scheduled events.

👉 "Most people pay a monthly fee for their mobile."

👉 "The computer system updates every Monday."

👉 "The office doesn't open on Wednesday afternoons."

Use 2 🏢

Permanent Situations

We use the Present Simple for long-term states or general truths that do not change quickly.

👉 "This company offers free email accounts."

👉 "My family live by the sea."

👉 "My mobile plan doesn't give free calls."

Forming Present Simple Sentences

We use the base form of the verb. He / She / It forms add -s / -es.

Positive (+)

I / You / We / They like pop music.

He / She / It likes pop music. (+s)

Negative (-)

I / You / We / They don't like social media.

He / She / It doesn't like social media.

Questions (?)

Do you like pop music?
✓ Yes, I do. / ✗ No, I don't.

Does he like pop music?
✓ Yes, he does. / ✗ No, he doesn't.

Present Continuous

B. Present Continuous Uses & Inversion

Formed with be (am/is/are) + -ing form for current or temporary actions.

Use 1 ⚡

Happening Right Now

To describe activities taking place at the exact moment of speaking.

👉 "The manager is updating the website."
Use 2 ⏳

Temporary Situations

To describe actions or projects happening around now, but for a limited time.

👉 "Dr Brown's working on a new system this summer."

⚡ Present Continuous Structure Formulas

Positive (+)

I'm / You're / He's / She's / It's / We're / They're + -ing form

"They're working hard."

Negative (-)

I'm not / You aren't / He isn't / She isn't / We aren't + -ing form

"He isn't living here."

Questions (?) Subject-Verb Inversion

Am / Is / Are + subject + -ing form?

"Are you working at the moment?"

🧠

C. State Verbs (Non-Continuous)

State verbs describe conditions/states, not actions, and are not normally used in a continuous form.

Group 1

Emotions & Mental States

Verbs that express thoughts, feelings, or preferences:

agree • disagree • hate • like • mind • need • prefer • promise • seem • understand • want
Group 2

The Senses

Verbs that describe sensory perceptions:

feel • hear • see • smell • sound • taste
Group 3

Owning & Possession

Verbs describing ownership or relationships:

belong to • have • own
⚠️

State Verb Guardrail Example

❌ Incorrect: "I am wanting a new phone."
✅ Correct: "I want a new phone." (Use Present Simple for state verbs!)
