A. Defining Relative Clauses

Used to identify, define, or classify a noun or pronoun in a sentence.

Function 🔍

Identify & Classify Nouns

Defining relative clauses give essential information about a noun. Without this clause, the sentence would be incomplete or unclear. No commas are used!

👉 "Happiness is an emotion which makes us feel good."

(= Happiness is an emotion. It makes us feel good.)

Register 📝

Using "that" vs. "who / which"

In spoken and informal English, we frequently use that instead of which or who. In formal writing, which and who are preferred.

💬 Informal: "The research that the professor gave us..."

📜 Formal: "The research which the professor gave us..."

Subject / Object

B. Relative Pronoun Functions & Omission Rules

Understand when relative pronouns act as subject vs object, and when they can be omitted (Ø).

1. Subject Pronoun 👤

Subject of the Relative Clause

Used when the relative pronoun is the subject performing the action in the relative clause.

👉 "A bucket list is something which can motivate us to experience new things."

(= A bucket list is a thing. It can motivate us...)

⚠️ CRITICAL TRAP! Do NOT repeat subject pronoun!

❌ Incorrect: "A person who is stressed he has high blood pressure."

✅ Correct: "A person who is stressed has high blood pressure."

2. Object Pronoun 📦

Object of the Relative Clause

Used when the relative pronoun is the object receiving the action.

👉 "We have to read the research report that the professor gave us."

(= We have to read the research report. The professor gave it to us.)

💡 GOLDEN RULE: Pronoun Omission (Ø)

We CAN omit the relative pronoun when it is the object!

👉 "Embarrassment is a feeling (that / Ø) we don't want to experience."

👤 Subject Relative Pronouns
  • Function: Replaces subject pronoun (he, she, it, they) inside the clause.
  • Omission Rule: ❌ NEVER omit a subject relative pronoun!
  • Example: "A person who lives next door is helpful."
📦 Object Relative Pronouns
  • Function: Replaces object pronoun (him, her, it, them) inside the clause.
  • Omission Rule: ✅ CAN be omitted completely (Ø) in defining clauses.
  • Example: "The report (that) the professor wrote..."
