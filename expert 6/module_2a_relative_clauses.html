Relative Clauses

Master defining relative clauses, subject vs. object relative pronouns, pronoun omission rules, and whose / where / when!

Clause

A. Defining Relative Clauses

Used to identify or classify a noun or pronoun in a sentence.

Function 🔍

Identify & Classify Nouns

Defining relative clauses give essential information about a noun. Without this clause, the sentence would be incomplete or unclear.

👉 "Happiness is an emotion which makes us feel good."

(= Happiness is an emotion. It makes us feel good.)

Formal vs Informal 📝

Using "that" vs. "who/which"

In spoken and informal English, we frequently use that instead of which or who. In more formal texts, which and who are preferred.

💬 Informal: "The research that the professor gave us..."

📜 Formal: "The research which the professor gave us..."

⚡ Essential Concept: Combining Two Sentences

Two Separate Sentences

1. Happiness is an emotion.

2. It makes us feel good.

Joined with Relative Clause

👉 "Happiness is an emotion which makes us feel good."

Subject / Object

B. Relative Pronoun Functions & Omission Rules

Understanding when relative pronouns act as subject vs. object, and when they can be omitted.

1. Subject Relative Pronoun 👤

Subject of the Relative Clause

Used when the relative pronoun is the subject performing the action in the relative clause.

👉 "A bucket list is something which can motivate us to experience new things."

(= A bucket list is a thing. It can motivate us...)

⚠️ CRITICAL TRAP! Do NOT repeat subject pronoun!

❌ Incorrect: "A person who is stressed he has high blood pressure."

✅ Correct: "A person who is stressed has high blood pressure."

2. Object Relative Pronoun 📦

Object of the Relative Clause

Used when the relative pronoun is the object receiving the action.

👉 "We have to read the research report that the professor gave us."

(= We have to read the research report. The professor gave it to us.)

💡 GOLDEN RULE: Pronoun Omission

We CAN omit the relative pronoun when it is the object!

👉 "Embarrassment is a feeling (that) we don't want to experience."

⚡ Subject vs. Object Relative Pronouns Quick Guide

Role Function in Clause Can it be omitted? Example Sentence
Subject Pronoun Followed by a verb (e.g. who is...) ❌ NEVER Omit "A person who is stressed..."
Object Pronoun Followed by subject + verb (e.g. we don't want...) ✅ CAN Omit "Embarrassment is a feeling (that) we don't want..."
🔗

C. Relative Pronouns & Adverbial Connectors

Matching pronouns to people, things, possessions, places, and times.

Things / Objects

which / that

Used for objects, ideas, or non-human items:

"A bucket list is something which / that can motivate us."
People

who / that

Used for people and individuals:

"A person who / that is stressed has high blood pressure."
Possessions

whose

Used to show ownership or relationship:

"A person whose body language is negative gives a bad impression."
Places

where

Connects to locations or geographical settings:

"Places where people are noisy can be very stressful."
Times / Dates

when

Connects to time expressions, days, or occasions:

"A time when people may feel happy is on their graduation day."
⚠️

Note on "When" and "Where"

Sentences with when and where are technically adverbial clauses rather than relative clauses, but they act in a virtually identical way to link descriptions to places and times!
