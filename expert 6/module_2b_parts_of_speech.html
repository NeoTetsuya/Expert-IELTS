Parts of Speech Cheat Sheet

1. Noun Object, Person, Place, Idea
2. Verb Action or State
3. Adjective Describes a Noun
4. Adverb How, Where, When
5. Preposition Time & Location
6. Pronoun Replaces a Noun
7. Conjunction Links Clauses / Ideas
8. Article a, an, the
Noun 📦

Describing Entities & Ideas

A noun describes an object, person, thing, place, or abstract idea.

"Confidence is an important quality."

"A lack of confidence can affect a person’s career."

Verb ⚡

Actions, States & Auxiliaries

A verb describes an action or a state. Auxiliary verbs help form tenses.

Action: "Some people speak very confidently."

State: "We feel confident if we look good."

Auxiliary + Main Verb: "I’ve gained confidence." / "She’s improving her skills."
Adjective 🎨

Modifying Nouns

tells us what kind or how many. It can come before or after the noun.

After 'be': "It is difficult to express emotions."

Before noun: "Expressing emotions can be difficult work."

"Confidence is an important quality."

Adverb 🚀

How, Where & When

Gives extra info about how, where, or when an action happens. Placed before/after verbs.

Where/How: "People often keep their emotions inside."

How: "The percentage of happy people is rising slowly."

How: "Analyse data carefully."

Preposition 📍

Time & Location Markers

Gives information about time and place. Usually comes before a noun or number.

"Looking at art can cause strong emotions."

"Listening to a sad song can make you feel depressed."

Pronoun 👤

Replacing Nouns

Replaces a noun (e.g. she, they, them, ours, him, this, these). Sentences often start with them.

"I’m not happy with the professor. He shouted at us today."

"You’ve made me feel very guilty."

Conjunction 🔗

Linking Ideas & Clauses

Links ideas in a sentence by joining two clauses (e.g., because, although, so, and, but).

"It’s good to show emotions because it releases stress."

"Although it is not good for health, keeping emotions inside can be better."

Article 🔤

a, an, the

A small word that precedes a noun to indicate specificity or generality.

Indefinite: "Fear is a common emotion."

Before vowel: "There’s an interesting research report here."

Definite: "The feelings we experience can be varied."
