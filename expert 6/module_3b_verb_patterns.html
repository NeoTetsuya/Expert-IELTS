Verb Patterns

Master when to follow a verb with an -ing form, a to-infinitive, or both!

The -ing Form (Gerund)

Using a verb like a noun to describe an activity in a general way.

A. Subject / Object 🏛️

As Subject or Object

Functions as the main noun in the sentence when talking about an action generally.

"Banking is less popular than it was in 2000."

"Working and studying at the same time can be hard."

B. After Prepositions 🔗

After Prepositions

When a verb follows a preposition (about, of, in, at, for, by), it MUST take -ing.

"I’m not happy about working late."

"Brian’s nervous about meeting his boss."

C. Specific Verbs ⭐

After Certain Verbs

Certain main verbs always trigger an -ing form on the verb that follows.

"The researchers suggested introducing higher taxes."

"The project involved building a new centre."

Infinitives (to + base verb)

The base form of a verb, usually preceded by 'to'.

A. After Adjectives 🎨

After Adjectives

We use to + infinitive following descriptive adjectives.

"It’s difficult to identify the right person for the job."

"It was hard to remember the company’s name."

B. Specific Verbs 📋

After Certain Verbs

Many verbs naturally connect to the next action with to + verb.

"The speakers agreed to disagree."

"The results appeared to show a difference in sales."

C. Purpose 🎯

To Express Purpose

Explains why someone does an action (answers "For what reason?").

"They separated into two groups to get the work done faster."

"The figures were checked to make sure they were correct."

Verbs Followed by -ing or Infinitive

Some verbs accept both structures. In some cases, there is little difference; in others, the meaning changes significantly.

Interactive Meaning Inspector

Compare Dual-Pattern Verbs

Pattern 1: + -ing Form

"He began talking."

Virtually the same meaning as the infinitive form. Describes entering into the activity of talking.

Pattern 2: + To-Infinitive

"He began to talk."

Identical or almost identical in meaning for 'begin' and 'start'.

Minimal Difference

begin & start

Both -ing and infinitive can be used interchangeably with no real change in meaning.

"He began talking."

"He began to talk."

Important Difference

stop, remember, forget

-ing refers to the action itself or a past memory. The infinitive refers to stopping in order to do an action, or remembering a duty.

"He stopped working." (Quit the activity)

"He stopped to work." (Paused to start working)
