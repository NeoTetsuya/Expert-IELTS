A1. Modal Verbs & Adverbs for Future Probability

Use modal combinations to signal your exact degree of certainty about future events.

100% Certainty 💯

will definitely (not) + infinitive

Used when you are 100% sure that something will or will not happen in the future.

👉 "Technology will definitely change the way we learn a foreign language."

👉 "We will definitely not lose our access to basic education."

~80% Quite Sure 📈

will probably (not) + infinitive

Used when you are quite sure, though not completely 100% certain.

👉 "Cars will probably not fly before 2050."

👉 "Online universities will probably become more popular."

~50% Weak Possibility 🎲

will possibly (not) + infinitive

Expresses a moderate possibility, but you state that you are not sure.

👉 "Automation will possibly reduce physical labor jobs."

30-50% Uncertain 🔮

might (not) / may (not) + infinitive

Used when something is possible, but you are not sure.

👉 "Our working lives may not be so different in 2030."

👉 "Space tourism might become affordable for some."

⚡ Future Probability Scale Quick Reference

Structure Meaning / Certainty Level Example Sentence
will definitely (not) + V I'm 100% sure. Technology will definitely change language learning.
will probably (not) + V I'm quite sure (~80%). Cars will probably not fly before 2050.
will possibly (not) + V It's possible, but I'm not sure (~50%). AI will possibly replace simple routine tasks.
might (not) / may (not) + V It's possible, but I'm not sure (~30-50%). Working lives may not be so different in 2030.

A2. Academic & Formal Probability Structures

Particularly common in academic writing because they present a formal, objective style.

1. Noun Phrases 🏛️

There is a [adj] chance / possibility that...

Quantifies probability using abstract nouns like chance or possibility.

  • Small Possibility: 👉 "There is a small chance that we will all have microchips in our brains."
  • Strong Possibility: 👉 "There is a strong possibility that printed books will disappear."
2. Adjective Phrases 📊

It is [quite / highly] likely that...

Uses dummy it paired with probability adjectives like likely.

  • Weak / Moderate Probability: 👉 "It is quite likely that more people will own their own home."
  • Strong Probability: 👉 "It is highly likely that cities will become overcrowded."
3. Personal / Direct Structure 🎓

[Subject] + be likely to + infinitive

Focuses directly on the subject, followed by be (is/are) likely to + bare infinitive.

👉 "More people are likely to learn computer programming at school."

👉 "Global temperatures are likely to rise over the next decade."

⚡ Academic Style Tip

Academic writers avoid over-assertive claims (such as "This will definitely happen") and prefer hedged formal phrases like "There is a strong possibility that..." or "It is highly likely that..." to sound objective and cautious!

B. Present Probability & Deductions

Expressing how sure or unsure we are about current states and situations.

1. Positive Certainty

must + infinitive

Used when we are sure that something is true based on evidence.

👉 "The scientists must be excited about their latest findings."

(= I'm sure they are excited.)

2. Negative Certainty

can't / cannot + infinitive

Used when we are sure that something is NOT possible.

👉 "Life on other planets cannot exist without water."

(= I'm sure it's impossible.)

3. Present Possibility

might / may / could + infinitive

Used when we think something is possible, but we are not sure.

👉 "Scientists might be able to cure serious illnesses."

👉 "Researchers may find a cure soon."

⚠️

Page 61 Deduction Guardrail: Must vs. Can't

• For positive certainty in present deductions, use must (e.g., "They must be happy.")
• For negative certainty in present deductions, use can't / cannot (NOT mustn't) (e.g., "That story can't be true!")
