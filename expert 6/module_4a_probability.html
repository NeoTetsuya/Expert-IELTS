A1. Modal Verbs & Adverbs for Future Probability

Use modal combinations to signal your exact degree of certainty about future events.

100% Certainty 💯

will definitely (not) + infinitive

Used when you are 100% sure that something will or will not happen in the future.

👉 "Technology will definitely change how we learn."

👉 "We definitely won't lose our access to basic education."

~80% Quite Sure 📈

will probably (not) + infinitive

Used when you are quite sure, though not completely 100% certain.

👉 "Cars will probably not fly before 2050."

👉 "Online degrees will probably become more common."

⚡ Academic Style & Hedging Tip

Academic writers avoid over-assertive claims (like "This will definitely happen") and prefer hedged formal phrases like "There is a strong possibility that..." or "It is highly likely that..." to remain objective and scientifically cautious!

Page 61 Deduction Guardrail: Must vs. Can't

• For positive certainty in present deductions, use must (e.g., "They must be happy.")
• For negative certainty in present deductions, use can't / cannot (NOT mustn't) (e.g., "That story can't be true!")

🔮 Modal Adverb Structures
  • Style: Direct & concise modal verb + adverb combinations.
  • Adverb Position: Positive ➔ AFTER will (will definitely) | Negative ➔ BEFORE won't (definitely won't).
  • Example: "Technology will definitely change education."
📜 Academic Formal Structures
  • Style: Objective, cautious, and hedged reporting style.
  • Key Formulas: "There is a strong chance that...", "It is highly likely that...", "[Subject] is likely to...".
  • Example: "It is highly likely that cities will expand."
