Future Forms

Master the nuances between will, be going to, and the Present Continuous for predictions, intentions, and arrangements!

Predictions & Intentions

How to express what you think, what you see, or what you plan to do in the future.

A. Will + Infinitive 🧠

Predictions: Thoughts & Beliefs

Used when predictions are based on personal opinions, expectations, or general beliefs.

"People will study technology more at school."

"Researchers will help us to understand the world better."

B. Going To + Infinitive 👁️

Predictions: Present Evidence

Used when you have clear physical evidence right now in front of you or in data.

"The rain is going to get heavier later."

(Evidence = looking at current satellite photos)

C. Going To + Infinitive 🎯

Intentions & Future Plans

Used for personal plans or decisions made prior to speaking (no fixed setup yet).

"We’re going to do an experiment into this topic."

(Decision made, but details aren't booked yet)

Quick Formula Cheat Sheet

will + base verb

Subject + will + v1 (e.g. She will arrive tomorrow.)

be (am/is/are) + going to + base verb

Subject + am/is/are + going to + v1 (e.g. They are going to study.)

Present Continuous for Future

Using present continuous structure (am/is/are + -ing) to express fixed future arrangements.

Fixed Arrangements 📆

Organised & Agreed Events

Refers to something that is already organized, confirmed, or written down in your calendar/diary.

"Experts are meeting on Friday to present their papers."

"Dr Smith is giving a talk on space travel next week."

Key Characteristics 🤝

What makes it an Arrangement?

Arrangements usually involve another person, a set time, a location, or tickets bought!

✔️ A specific date or time is set (e.g., on Friday, next week)

✔️ Two or more people have agreed on the meeting

✔️ Something physical is booked or scheduled

Key Difference

📅 Organized & Agreed

The professor has confirmed meetings scheduled with students.

Arrangement (Present Continuous)

Present Continuous

Focuses on the scheduled time, date, or confirmed booking.

"I’m flying to Paris on Tuesday." (Tickets are booked!)

Intention (be going to)

Be Going To

Focuses on the decision or goal, but nothing is officially booked or agreed yet.

"The professor is going to tell us more about his science award some time soon." (Intention, no date booked)
