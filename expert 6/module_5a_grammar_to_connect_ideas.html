This / These

A. Using "This" and "These" to Connect Sentences

Replaces singular/plural nouns or combines with summary nouns/synonyms to link ideas smoothly.

1. Singular Pronoun ☝️

this (standalone pronoun)

Replaces a singular noun, clause, or situation mentioned in the previous sentence.

👉 "There was an increase in gallery visitors. This was largely due to new opening hours."

2. This + Summary Noun 🔄

this + [Summary Noun / Synonym]

Combines this with a summary noun or synonym to categorize a previous concept clearly.

👉 "They held the competition every two years. This change gave artists more time."

⚡ Academic Cohesion Tip: "This + Summary Noun"

In academic essays, using This + noun (e.g. This decision, This trend, This reduction, This change) is significantly clearer and more sophisticated than using a bare "This" alone!

🔗 This / These (Immediate Reference)
  • Scope: Connects to ideas or items mentioned in the immediately preceding sentence.
  • Academic Style: Strongly prefer This + summary noun (e.g. This change).
  • Example: "They changed the timetable. This decision was welcomed."
⏳ That / Those (Distant / Comparison)
  • Scope: Connects to historical/geographical distance OR noun replacement in comparisons.
  • Human Groups: Use those who as formal replacement for people who.
  • Example: "Sales of books grew faster than those of magazines."
