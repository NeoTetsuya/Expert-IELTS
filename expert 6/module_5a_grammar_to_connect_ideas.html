Grammar to Connect Ideas

Master cohesive references using this & these, that & those, those who, do so & modals, and such!

This / These

A. Using "This" and "These" to Connect Sentences

Replaces singular/plural nouns or combines with synonyms to repeat ideas smoothly.

1. Singular Noun ☝️

this (standalone pronoun)

Replaces a singular noun, clause, or situation mentioned in the previous sentence.

👉 "There was an increase in numbers of visitors to the gallery. This was largely due to the new opening hours."

(= This increase was due to new hours.)

2. This + Synonym 🔄

this + [Synonym / Noun]

Combines this with a summary noun or synonym to summarize a full idea.

👉 "Organisers decided to hold the art competition every two years, rather than every year. This change was made to give artists more time to prepare."

('This change' summarizes holding it every 2 years)

3. Plural Noun 👥

these (standalone pronoun)

Replaces a plural noun mentioned previously.

👉 "There were a lot of complaints about the exhibition. Most of these related to just one painting."

(= Most of these complaints...)

4. These + Synonym 👥🏷️

these + [Plural Synonym]

Combines these with a plural synonym to re-categorize a group.

👉 "The number of teenagers who regularly purchase music decreased. The availability of free music online was the main reason reported by these young people."

('these young people' replaces 'teenagers')

⚡ Academic Cohesion Tip: "This + Summary Noun"

In IELTS/academic essays, using This + noun (e.g. This decision, This trend, This reduction, This change) is significantly clearer and more sophisticated than using a bare "This" alone!

That / Those

B & C. That, Those & "Those Who"

When to use that/those instead of this/these, and using those who.

1. Distance (Time/Space) 🏛️

Distant Reference

Use that (sing) or those (plural) when the reference is far away in history, time, or geographic space.

👉 "Hatra contains fine examples of architecture. That region of modern-day Iran was a major centre..."

👉 "Michelangelo painted the Sistine Chapel from 1508–1512. At that time, it wasn't unusual..."

2. Comparisons 📊

Comparisons & "Same As"

Use that / those following a comparative form or the same as to avoid repeating the noun.

👉 "Sales of paintings rose faster than those of statues." (= sales of statues)

👉 "The statistics for art students... were the same as for those on postgraduate programmes."

Section C: "Those Who" 👥

those who = people who

Use those who as a formal, elegant replacement for people who.

👉 "Many people dislike visiting art galleries. However, those who enjoy going there visit regularly."

👉 "...separated into those who were in full-time work and those who were not."

⚡

D. Do So, Substitution & Modals

Avoiding repetitive verb phrases using do so or short auxiliaries / modals.

1. Verb Substitution 📝

do so (replaces "do it / action")

In formal academic English, use do so / doing so / did so instead of do it / doing it.

👉 "The gallery owners decided to show a much wider range of paintings. By doing so, they attracted a wider audience."

(= By showing a wider range of paintings...)

2. Auxiliary / Modal Shortening ✂️

can / do / are (short form)

Use an auxiliary or modal verb (e.g. can, do, are) in place of repeating a whole verb phrase.

👉 "Many people think they can't understand art, but they can."

(= ...but they can understand art.)

🏛️

E. Using "Such" to Replace Nouns

Formulas for singular countable vs. plural & uncountable nouns.

Singular Countable 1️⃣

such + a (adj) + singular noun

Used when referring back to a singular countable noun or event. Must include a/an!

👉 "The government decided to increase the number of hours spent on art in primary schools. Such a big decision was always going to create disagreement."

Plural / Uncountable 🔢

such + (adj) + plural/uncountable noun

Used with plural or uncountable nouns. Do NOT include a/an!

👉 "The fashion industry is worth around $1.2 trillion each year. Many people think that such large sums of money should be used for better purposes."
