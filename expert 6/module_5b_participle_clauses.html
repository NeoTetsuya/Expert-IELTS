Participle Clauses (-ing & -ed)

Streamline your academic writing! Learn how to reduce defining relative clauses using present participles (-ing) for active verbs and past participles (-ed / V3) for passive verbs.

Active Verbs → Present Participles (-ing)

Replace defining relative clauses containing active verbs to write concisely.

Step 1 ✂️

Omit Relative Pronoun

Remove words like who, which, or that completely from the clause.

The people who practised...

The number of people who make...

Step 2 🔄

Change Verb to -ing

Change the active verb to its present participle (-ing) form.

practised → practising

make → making

Key Rule ⏰

Works for ANY Tense

The verb does not need to be in the present tense! Works equally for past and present actions.

✔️ Past action (practised) → practising

✔️ Present action (make) → making

Academic Sentence Transformations

Active Verbs
Example 1 (Past Tense):
Full Clause: "The people who practised a new hobby reported feeling happier."
Participle Clause: "The group practising a new hobby reported feeling happier."
Example 2 (Present Tense):
Full Clause: "The number of people who make their own clothes has gone down."
Participle Clause: "The number of people making their own clothes has gone down."

Passive Verbs → Past Participles (-ed / V3)

Omit the relative pronoun and the auxiliary verb be in passive relative clauses.

Reduction Rule ✂️

Omit Relative Pronoun + 'be'

When the relative clause is in passive voice (pronoun + was/were/is/are + past participle), drop both the pronoun and the verb be.

The leisure centre which was used...

The novel which was based...

Resulting Form ✨

Keep the Past Participle Only

The past participle (involved, eaten, given, used, based) remains to lead the reduced clause.

"The leisure centre used for most activities..."

"The novel based on true events..."

Academic Sentence Transformations

Passive Verbs
Example 1:
Full Clause: "The leisure centre which was used for most after-school activities closed down."
Participle Clause: "The leisure centre used for most after-school activities closed down."
Example 2:
Full Clause: "The novel which was based on true events has been made into a film."
Participle Clause: "The novel based on true events has been made into a film."

Active Verb Pattern

Active → Present Participle (-ing)

Applies to actions performed by the subject, regardless of past or present tense.

who make → making

which contain → containing

Passive Verb Pattern

Passive → Past Participle (-ed / V3)

Applies when the subject receives the action (contains auxiliary be + V3).

which was used → used

which was based → based
