Present Perfect Simple vs. Past Simple

Master connecting past actions to the present with Present Perfect Simple, contrasting finished past events with Past Simple, and using for / since, just / already / yet!

have/has + V3

A. Present Perfect Simple Core Functions

Connects a past action or state to the present moment.

1. Past Action → Present Result 🔗

Indefinite Past Time

Action happened at an indefinite time in the past, but the result is important now.

👉 "I've been to university."

(= I went in the past, exact time is unstated.)

👉 "The world population has increased significantly in recent years."

(= Result now: a high world population.)

2. Continuing / Unfinished 📈

Started in Past, Continues Now

Action began in the past and is still ongoing. Usually paired with for or since.

👉 "Global warming has been an issue for the last two decades."

👉 "Technology has developed significantly since industrialisation."

Past vs Perfect

B. Past Simple vs. Present Perfect Simple

Finished past time vs. Unfinished or indefinite past connection.

Past Simple 📅

Finished Action at Specific Time

Used when an action started and completely finished at a definite time in the past.

👉 "The company became more energy efficient in 2015."

👉 "Oil prices fell last year."

Common Past Simple Time Words:

ago • at 4 o'clock • before 2010 • in 2016 • last week • yesterday

Present Perfect ⚡

Indefinite Time / Present Connection

Used when the exact time is unstated, or when the action continues into or affects the present.

👉 "Oil prices have fallen recently."

(= Recent fall, effect is felt now.)

👉 "They have lived here for 10 years."

(= They still live here now.)

Common Present Perfect Time Words:

for • since • so far • recently • ever • never • already • yet

For / Since

C. For vs. Since & Key Time Expressions

Precision rules for time indicators used with Present Perfect Simple.

1. FOR ⏱️

Duration / Length of Time

Use FOR to state how long an action or state has lasted.

👉 "Global warming has been an issue for the last two decades."

👉 "They have lived in London for ten years / for a long time."

2. SINCE 📌

Starting Point in Time

Use SINCE to state the exact starting moment when an action began.

👉 "Technology has developed significantly since industrialisation."

👉 "She has worked at the university since 2018 / since last January."

📅 Past Simple (Finished Time)
  • Focus: Completed action at a specific, finished time in the past.
  • Time Indicators: yesterday, in 2015, two years ago, last week.
  • Example: "The company became energy efficient in 2015."
⏳ Present Perfect (Present Connection)
  • Focus: Action in past with a present result, or action continuing up to now.
  • Time Indicators: for, since, recently, so far, just, already, yet.
  • Example: "Oil prices have fallen recently."
