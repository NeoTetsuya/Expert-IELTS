A, B & C. Passive Tense Transformations

Forming passive structures across Present, Past, and Future tenses.

A. Present Passive 🌱

Simple & Continuous

Describes present habits, facts, or actions in progress.

  • Present Simple Passive: am / is / are + V3 👉 "A machine is used to clean the vegetables."
  • Present Continuous Passive: am / is / are being + V3 👉 "The potatoes are being checked to make sure they meet standards."
B. Past Passive 📅

Simple & Continuous

Describes completed past actions or actions that were in progress in the past.

  • Past Simple Passive: was / were + V3 👉 "The food was placed on the counter."
  • Past Continuous Passive: was / were being + V3 👉 "The accident happened when the barbecue was being lit."
C. Future Passive 🔮

Will Passive & Going To Passive

Will Passive (will be + V3)

Future predictions or conditional consequences:

"Food crops will be destroyed if the storm hits the south."

Going to Passive (am/is/are going to be + V3)

Planned future events:

"The crops are going to be harvested soon."

D & Overview: Modals & Why We Use Passive

Focusing on the action rather than the agent, and using passive with modal verbs.

Overview 💡

When to Use Passive Voice

Use passive when you want to make the action more important than the person who did it, or when the person is unknown / not important.

🏃 Active: "The farmer harvests the crops in autumn."

⚙️ Passive: "The crops are harvested in autumn."

🏃 Active: "The waiter removes plates from the table."

⚙️ Passive: "The plates are removed from the table."

D. Modals & Passive 🔒

modal verb + be + past participle

Combine modal verbs (can, may, must, should, etc.) directly with be + past participle (V3).

👉 "Bread can be made with a variety of crop types."

👉 "Corn crops may be harvested early if fine weather continues."

E. Subject and Verb Agreement Rules

Precision rules for choosing singular vs. plural verbs with quantifiers and collective nouns.

1. Singular Verbs 1️⃣

Use Singular Verbs With:

  • Singular & Uncountable Nouns:
    👉 "More and more fast food is being eaten these days."
  • everyone, everybody, every, someone, somebody, some, no one, nobody, no, each, either
  • The amount of, An/The increase:
    👉 "There has been an increase in the amount of water which is being used."
2. Plural Verbs 🔢

Use Plural Verbs With:

  • Countable Plural Nouns
  • None, All, Neither, A/The number of:
    👉 "None of them are good enough to eat."
    👉 "Neither of them provide an effective solution."
    👉 "A number of homes have been cut off from the water supply."

3. Collective Nouns 👥

Group vs. Individual Focus (Dual Option)

With collective nouns like a group, a team, a family, a company, you can use EITHER singular or plural verbs depending on your focus:

Singular Verb = Focus on Group as a Single Unit

👉 "My family has been given a new home."

👉 "A team of farmers has been rewarded for their good food."

Plural Verb = Focus on Individual Members of Group

👉 "My family have been given a new home."

👉 "A team of farmers have been rewarded for their hard work."

🏃 Active Voice (Doer First)
  • Focus: Emphasizes WHO performs the action (Subject = Doer).
  • Structure: Subject + Active Verb + Object.
  • Example: "The farmer harvests the crops in autumn."
⚙️ Passive Voice (Action / Receiver First)
  • Focus: Emphasizes WHAT happens or WHO receives the action (Agent omitted or secondary).
  • Structure: Receiver + Auxiliary BE + Past Participle (V3) (+ by Agent).
  • Example: "The crops are harvested in autumn."
