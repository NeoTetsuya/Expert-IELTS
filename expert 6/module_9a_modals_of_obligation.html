Modals of Obligation

Master Present Obligation (have to vs. must), Past & Future Obligation (had to, will have to), and Formal Obligation Expressions!

Present

A. Present Obligation & Necessity

Expressing external rules, personal decisions, prohibitions, and advice in the present.

1. External Decision 📋

has / have to + infinitive

Talks about an obligation that exists because of a decision made by another person (supervisor, doctor, rule, law).

👉 "I have to stop eating so much sugar." (My dietician told me.)

👉 "She has to finish the report before tomorrow." (Her supervisor told her.)

2. Internal Necessity 💡

must + infinitive

Talks about an obligation that the speaker thinks is necessary or important.

👉 "I must send this invitation out today." (Event is next week.)

👉 "Children must spend more time doing sport." (I think it's important.)

3. No Obligation 🎉

don't have to / don't need to

States that an action is not necessary (optional):

👉 "You don't have to exercise every day, usually three times a week is fine."
4. Prohibition 🛑

mustn't + infinitive

States that an action is not permitted or forbidden:

👉 "You mustn't copy material from the internet for your end-of-term paper."
5, 6 & 7. Mild & Possible 💬

should / need to / can

Advice, general necessity & possibility:

  • should: Mild obligation/advice ("He should speak to his tutor.")
  • need to: General necessity ("He needs to give his work in.")
  • can: Possibility ("He can leave now if he wants.")

Past & Future

B & C. Past and Future Obligation

How obligation changes across past and future timeframes.

B. Past Obligation ⏳

had to / didn't have to

had to is the past form of BOTH have to and must!

  • (+) Past Obligation (had to + infinitive): 👉 "I had to stay up late last night to prepare for the meeting."
  • (-) No Past Obligation (didn't have to + infinitive): 👉 "We didn't have to give back the question paper after the test."
C. Future Obligation 🔮

will have to / won't have to

Expresses future requirements or absence of future obligations.

  • (+) Future Obligation (will have to + infinitive): 👉 "The company will have to reduce their costs next year."
  • (-) No Future Obligation (won't have to + infinitive): 👉 "Science students won't have to attend the language class."

⚡ CRITICAL GRAMMAR RULE: Past Form of Must

Remember that must has no past tense form! The past form of both must and have to is strictly had to! (e.g., "I must go today" → "I had to go yesterday").

D. Other Expressions of Obligation

Formal phrases expressing obligation often tested in academic reading and writing exams.

Phrase 1

make something possible

Enables an outcome or gives capability.

👉 "The large donation made the new health centre possible."
Phrase 2

permit (be permitted to)

Formal equivalent for allow / can.

👉 "Scientists were not permitted to work on the new cure."
Phrase 3

be necessary (it is/was necessary to)

Formal phrase for necessity or requirement.

👉 "It became necessary to change the way people ate."
Phrase 4

be prohibited

Formal equivalent for mustn't / not allowed.

👉 "Travelling without a vaccination is prohibited."

📋 Have to vs. Must
  • have to: External rule / order from someone else (boss, doctor, law).
  • must: Speaker's own belief or internal feeling of necessity.
  • Past Form: BOTH become had to in the past!
🛑 Mustn't vs. Don't have to
  • mustn't: Forbidden / Prohibited / Not allowed.
  • don't have to: Not necessary / Optional / Up to you.
  • Contrast Example: "You mustn't cheat" (Forbidden) vs "You don't have to stay" (Optional).
