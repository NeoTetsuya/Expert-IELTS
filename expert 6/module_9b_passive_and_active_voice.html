Passive and Active Voice

Master when to place the subject as the doer or the receiver of an action, and learn why intransitive verbs can never be used in the passive voice!

Understanding the Passive Voice

In passive voice sentences, the subject receives the action rather than performing it.

Core Concept 1 💊

Action Done TO the Subject

The grammatical subject of the sentence is the target or recipient of the action.

"The drug was invented in the 1930s."

"The patient was given antibiotics."

"The meat was tested for bacteria."

Core Concept 2 🔬

Focus on the Event / Outcome

Used when the event itself is more important than who or what caused it to happen.

"The origins of the gene will probably never be known."

"Thousands of medicines are tested every year."

Passive Voice Formula

be + Past Participle (V3)

Passive tense is formed by using the auxiliary verb be (in the appropriate tense) followed by the past participle (V3) of the main verb.

Present Simple Passive

is / are + tested

Past Simple Passive

was / were + invented

Future Passive

will be + known

Active Voice & Intransitive Verbs

Learn when the subject performs the action and why certain verbs CANNOT be used passively!

Active Voice 👤

Subject = Doer of Action

We use the active voice when the subject of the sentence is the person or thing performing the action.

"Alexander Fleming identified the bacteria." (Fleming performed the action)

"Professor Liu was studying bacteria in meat." (Professor Liu was performing the study)

⚠️ Critical Academic Rule

Intransitive Verbs = Active Voice ONLY!

Intransitive verbs do NOT take a direct object. Because there is no object to receive the action, they CANNOT be made passive! Common intransitive verbs:

appear
arrive
become
come
consist
cry
disappear
fall
go up
go down
happen
occur
remain
rise
work
increase

✔️ "The number of people who do no exercise at all has increased." (NOT has been increased)

✔️ "The popularity of regular exercise has fallen." (NOT was fallen)

✔️ "An unexpected error occurred during testing." (NOT was occurred)

Active vs. Passive Sentence Focus Switcher

Select scenarios below to inspect how subject focus and sentence structure change between active and passive forms!

Interactive Focus Switcher

Compare Sentence Structures

Active Voice (Subject = Doer)

"Alexander Fleming identified the bacteria in 1928."

Subject = "Alexander Fleming". Focus is on the scientist performing the action.

Passive Voice (Subject = Recipient)

"The bacteria was identified by Alexander Fleming in 1928."

Subject = "The bacteria". Focus shifts to the biological entity and event.

💡

Key Grammar Guardrail

Remember: If a verb is intransitive (like happen, increase, fall, arrive), it has NO passive version! Always keep it active.
