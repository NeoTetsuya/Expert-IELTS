Verb Formers

Creating Action Verbs

  • -ify / -fy: simplify, clarify, intensify, justify
  • -ize / -ise: summarize, prioritize, emphasize
  • -ate: formulate, cultivate, facilitate
  • -en: broaden, strengthen, sharpen, deepen
Formula: Adjective/Noun + Suffix → Verb
Noun Formers

Abstract & Agent Nouns

  • -tion / -sion: innovation, comprehension
  • -ity / -ty: capability, sustainability
  • -ness / -ment: awareness, development
  • -er / -or / -ist: researcher, benefactor, theorist
Formula: Verb/Adj + Suffix → Noun Concept
Adjective Formers

Descriptive Attributes

  • -able / -ible: viable, plausible, sustainable
  • -al / -ic: substantial, academic, economic
  • -ous / -ive: rigorous, lucrative, persuasive
  • -ful / -less: impactful, relentless
Formula: Noun/Verb + Suffix → Adjective
Prefix Nuance

Negative & Degree Prefixes

  • mis-: misunderstand, mislead, miscalculate
  • un- / dis-: unprecedented, disproportionate
  • in-/im-/ir-/il-: inevitable, impractical, irrelevant
  • over- / under-: overestimate, undervalue
Modifies meaning without changing part of speech.

Spelling Trick: The Assimilation Rule for Negative Prefix 'in-'

The negative prefix in- morphs phonetically based on the starting consonant of the root word to make pronunciation smoother:

Before P / M → IM-

possible → impossible

moral → immoral

Before R → IR-

responsible → irresponsible

rational → irrational

Before L → IL-

legal → illegal

logical → illogical

Before standard vowels/consonants → IN-

accurate → inaccurate

efficient → inefficient

Core Nominalization Formulas

Verb / Adjective Clause Nominalized Noun Phrase Academic Context Shift
"The population grew rapidly..." "The rapid growth of the population..." Verb + Adverb → Adjective + Noun
"Because prices fluctuated wildly..." "Due to wild price fluctuations..." Clause → Prepositional Noun Phrase
"The system is efficient..." "The efficiency of the system..." Adjective → Abstract Noun
"They analyzed data thoroughly..." "A thorough analysis of data..." Spelling root shift (analyze → analysis)

Stress Shift Conversion

Noun vs. Verb Stress Shift

Many two-syllable words change from Noun (stress on 1st syllable) to Verb (stress on 2nd syllable) without changing spelling:

’increase (N)

"There was a sharp increase."

in’crease (V)

"Prices will increase."

’present (N)

"He gave a present."

pre’sent (V)

"She will present data."

’conduct (N)

"His code of conduct."

con’duct (V)

"They conduct research."

Compound Adjectives

Hyphenated Academic Compounds

Combining words with hyphens creates powerful modifying adjectives before nouns:

Noun + Past Participle

technology-driven economy, state-funded healthcare, community-based initiative

Adverb / Adjective + Present Participle

far-reaching consequences, fast-growing sector, forward-thinking policy

Number + Noun (Singular)

a three-year study (NOT three-years), a ten-fold increase
