💡 Anaphoric Mechanism Breakdown:

Anaphoric Summary Noun Referencing

Elevate bare demonstratives (this, these) into Band 8.0+ summary noun phrases (*this disparity, such interventions, these developments*).

Bare Demonstrative Trap Band 6.0 Ambiguity ⚠️

Vague "This" or "These"

Using "This" alone to start a sentence can confuse readers about whether you mean the whole previous sentence, a specific noun, or the last action mentioned.

❌ Vague Unattached 'This':

"Urban schools receive double the funding of rural schools. This is a major concern."

❌ Vague Unattached 'These':

"Governments tax soft drinks and subsidize vegetables. These reduce obesity."

Band 8.0+ Encapsulation Precise Cohesion ✔️

Demonstrative + Abstract Summary Noun

Combine a demonstrative determiner (*this, these, such*) with an abstract noun that summarizes the entire preceding concept.

✔️ Band 8.0+ Precision:

"Urban schools receive double the funding of rural schools. This alarming financial disparity is a major concern."

✔️ Band 8.0+ Precision:

"Governments tax soft drinks and subsidize vegetables. Such fiscal measures promote public health."

Academic Summary Noun Classification Toolkit

Discrepancies & Problems

disparity, inequality, shortcoming, trend, dilemma

"This systemic disparity highlights..."

Actions & Solutions

intervention, initiative, reform, policy, strategy

"Such proactive initiatives encourage..."

Debates & Ideas

assertion, controversy, phenomenon, shift, perspective

"This paradigm shift indicates..."

Ambiguity & Dangling Antecedent Elimination

Detect and repair ambiguous pronoun references where two potential noun antecedents create confusion.

Dual Antecedent Confusion

Case A: Multiple Plural Nouns

❌ Ambiguous Sentence:

"The directors met with the consultants because they were concerned about performance."

→ Unclear: Who was concerned? The directors or the consultants?

✔️ Repaired for Clarity:

"Concerned about organizational performance, the directors met with the consultants."

Dangling 'Which' Scope

Case B: Sentential Relative Clause Scope

❌ Ambiguous Sentence:

"The government increased taxes on luxury vehicles, which angered citizens."

→ Did the vehicles anger citizens, or did the tax increase anger them?

✔️ Repaired for Clarity:

"The government increased taxes on luxury vehicles, a policy decision that angered citizens."
