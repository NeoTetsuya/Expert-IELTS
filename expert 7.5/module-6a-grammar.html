Correlative Conjunctions & Subject-Verb Agreement

Explore how paired conjunctions connect equal grammatical elements and dictate singular vs. plural verb agreement rules.

Additive Addition ALWAYS PLURAL VERB ✔️

Both... and

Joins two positive entities. Because it always combines two items together into a plural subject, the verb MUST be plural.

Subject Joining:

"Both the project lead and her senior analyst support the policy change."

Pronoun Equivalent:

"Both of them were present at the briefing."

Dual Negation PROXIMITY RULE ⚠️

Neither... nor

Negates two options simultaneously ("not A and not B"). Verb agreement follows the subject closest to the verb.

Singular Nearest Subject:

"Neither the directors nor the manager is willing to compromise."

Plural Nearest Subject:

"Neither the manager nor the directors are willing to compromise."

Exclusive Alternative PROXIMITY RULE ⚠️

Either... or

Presents a choice between two alternatives. Like *neither/nor*, verb agreement matches the noun closest to the verb.

Choice Formulation:

"Either additional subsidies or a direct tax reduction is necessary."

Action Choice:

"Municipalities must either expand public transit or face gridlock."

Emphatic Addition PARALLEL BALANCE ✨

Not only... but also

Emphasizes an unexpected second point. Requires strict grammatical parallelism between the two connected parts.

Standard Mid-Sentence Parallelism:

"The reform will not only boost GDP but also reduce unemployment."

Fronted Inversion Form:

"Not only does the policy boost GDP, but it also creates jobs."

💡 The Proximity Rule Formula

When using Neither... nor or Either... or to connect compound subjects, the verb agrees in number with the subject noun that is closest to the verb!

Singular Nearest = Singular Verb

"Neither the team members nor the supervisor was present."

Plural Nearest = Plural Verb

"Neither the supervisor nor the team members were present."

Grammatical Symmetry

Correlative Parallel Structure Rule

Elements connected by paired conjunctions (*both...and, not only...but also, either...or*) MUST belong to the exact same grammatical class (Noun + Noun, Verb + Verb, Prepositional Phrase + Prepositional Phrase).

❌ Faulty Unbalanced Structure:

"The manager is both energetic [Adjective] and has experience [Verb Phrase]."

✔️ Balanced Parallel Structure:

"The manager is both energetic [Adjective] and experienced [Adjective]."

Emphatic Inversion

Fronted Negative Adverb Inversion

Placing negative or limiting adverbs at the start of a clause forces auxiliary verb inversion (**Auxiliary + Subject + Verb**).

Standard Uninverted Order:

"The policy not only reduces emissions, but it also creates green jobs."

✔️ Band 8.5+ Inverted Order:

"Not only does the policy reduce emissions, but it also creates green jobs."

Academic Cohesion & Transition Vocabulary Toolkit

Categorized linking phrases to ensure smooth paragraph logic for IELTS Task 2 essays.

Cause & Consequence

Result & Effect Linkers

Connects an underlying cause to its direct logical result across sentence boundaries.

Transitions:

Consequently, Therefore, As a result, Accordingly, Thus

Sample Sentence:

"Tariffs were raised significantly; consequently, consumer demand plunged."

Contrast & Concession

Counter-Argumentation

Signals a pivot to a counter-argument, unexpected outcome, or balancing perspective.

Transitions:

However, Nevertheless, In contrast, Conversely, On the other hand

Sample Sentence:

"Initial costs are substantial. Nevertheless, long-term savings outweigh the outlay."

Addition & Elaboration

Reinforcement Linkers

Adds further supporting evidence or expands upon an established argument.

Transitions:

Furthermore, Moreover, In addition, Additionally, What is more

Sample Sentence:

"Public transit reduces emissions. Furthermore, it relieves traffic congestion."
