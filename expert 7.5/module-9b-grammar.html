Numerical Approximation & Range Modifiers

Learn how to place numerical approximation modifiers directly before data points, metrics, and measurements to express hedged scientific claims.

The Master Approximation Formula
1. Approximation Modifier approximately / roughly / in the region of
2. DATA POINT / NUMBER ★ 100°C / 45% / 2,500 UNITS
3. Temporal / Context Scope during the second trial phase

→ Combined Output: "The chamber reached in the region of 100°C during the second trial phase."

Before Numbers PRE-NUMERICAL POSITION ★

approximately / about / around / roughly

Positioned directly before a numeric metric or measurement to signal that the figure is close but not exact.

Temperature Measurement:

"The core temperature stabilized at approximately 100°C."

Proportional Percentage:

"Revenues expanded by roughly 25% over the fiscal year."

Academic Register BAND 8.0+ FORMULA ★

in the region of / in the vicinity of / on the order of

Sophisticated prepositional phrases used in formal scientific and economic reports to frame large estimates.

High-Value Financial Metric:

"Capital allocation was in the region of $5 million."

Large Population Metric:

"Annual urban migration is in the vicinity of 500,000 residents."

Directional Margin UPPER / LOWER LIMITS ★

just under / just over / well below / marginally above

Specifies whether an estimated value falls slightly above, slightly below, or substantially beneath a landmark figure.

Slightly Below Target (49%):

"Adoption rates reached just under 50% by Q4."

Slightly Above Target (51%):

"Expenditure rose marginally above $10,000."

Qualitative Modifier ATTRIBUTE MODIFIER ★

more or less / virtually / essentially / to a large extent

Modifies adjectives or state descriptions to indicate near-total equivalence or substantial alignment.

Near Equivalence:

"The two experimental prototypes are more or less identical in output."

Near Completion:

"The infrastructural overhaul is virtually complete."

Categorization & Open Series Language

Phrases for signaling unlisted continuation (*and so on, etc.*) and framing taxonomy classifications (*kinds of, types of, varieties of*).

End-of-List Markers SERIES CONTINUATION ★

and so on / etc. / among others / and the like

Signifies at the conclusion of a list that additional unlisted examples exist within the same categorical grouping.

End-of-List Extension:

"The recycling plant processes glass, paper, aluminum, and so on."

→ Indicates that other recyclable materials belong to the series.

Prepositional Sample Insertion:

"The study evaluated major European cities, among others."

Pre-Noun Framing CLASSIFICATION ★

kinds of / types of / categories of / varieties of

Placed before a general noun to indicate that the topic encompasses multiple subtypes or sub-classifications.

General Subtype Framing:

"The survey evaluated several kinds of / types of renewable energy installations."

Formal Taxonomy Framing:

"Researchers categorized the subjects into distinct categories of demographic profiles."

💡 Stylistic Rules for Lists & Categorization
❌ Avoid Redundant List Markers

Do NOT combine "for example, X, Y, etc." in the same sentence. Choose EITHER "for example, X and Y" OR "X, Y, etc."

✔️ Prefer 'and so on' or 'among others'

In formal academic writing, "and so on" or "among others" is often preferred over the abbreviation "etc."

Academic Data Indication & Representation Verbs

Replace generic reporting words (*shows*) with precise indicative verbs (*indicates, demonstrates, illustrates, highlights, conveys*).

Primary Data Linking

indicate / suggest / point to

Connects empirical data directly to a logical inference or underlying trend.

Sample Essay Formula:

"The survey results indicate that consumer confidence is recovering."

Visual Representation

illustrate / demonstrate / depict

Used in IELTS Task 1 overviews to describe what a graph, chart, or process diagram shows visually.

Sample Overview Formula:

"The diagram illustrates the multi-stage desalination process."

Key Feature Emphasis

highlight / underscore / accentuate

Draws explicit attention to the most prominent feature, peak, or outlier in a dataset.

Sample Detail Formula:

"The metrics highlight a sharp surge in solar power adoption after 2020."
