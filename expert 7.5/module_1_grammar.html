Strategy 1

Synonyms

Synonyms are words with identical or closely related meanings. Replacing words with synonyms prevents repetitive writing and raises your Lexical Resource score in academic exams.

Paraphrase Pair 1

pace ↔ rate

"improve your pace of learning" → "improve your rate of learning"

Paraphrase Pair 2

method ↔ approach / technique

💡 Context Tip: Ensure the synonym fits the specific academic collocations (e.g. rate of progress).
Strategy 2

Prefixes

Prefixes are letter combinations attached to the beginning of a root word to reverse or modify its meaning without rephrasing into long multi-word clauses.

Prefix 'mis-' ("wrongly")

won't understand → misunderstand

Prefix 'dis-' / 'un-' / 'in-' ("not")

not agree → disagree | not common → uncommon

💡 Context Tip: Using prefixes turns negative verb clauses into elegant single-word verbs or adjectives.
Strategy 3

Word Formation

Shift parts of speech (adjective → verb, noun → verb) using suffixes like -ify, -ize, -en, or -ate to convert wordy descriptions into concise active verbs.

Adjective → Verb (-ify)

make simpler → simplify

Noun → Verb (-ize / -ate)

make summary → summarize | give creation → create

💡 Context Tip: Replacing wordy phrases like "make a task simpler" with "simplify" streamlines academic sentences.

1

Simple Tenses (Present & Past)

Auxiliaries: do / does / did

Simple tenses state routine facts, habits, general truths, or single completed past events. Negatives and questions require auxiliary do/does/did + base verb.

Present Simple Question

"Do you go to school every day?"

Past Simple Negative

"I didn’t do my homework yesterday."

2

Continuous Tenses (Progressive)

Auxiliary: 'be' (am/is/are/was/were) + -ing

Emphasizes ongoing or in-progress activities occurring at a specific moment in time (now or at a specific past moment).

Present Continuous

"I’m learning to speak Hindi."

Past Continuous

"Ahmet wasn’t having lunch at 2p.m. yesterday."

3

Perfect Tenses

Auxiliary: 'have/has/had' + Past Participle (V3)

Links two points in time. Present Perfect links past action to present relevance; Past Perfect shows an action completed before another past event.

Present Perfect

"Huan hasn’t seen the teacher yet."

Past Perfect

"I hadn’t learnt any Greek words before I arrived in Athens."

4

Perfect Continuous Combination

Formula: have/has/had + been + -ing

Emphasizes the continuous duration or process of an activity leading up to a specific reference moment.

Present Perfect Continuous

"Daniel hasn’t been studying very much, so he probably won’t pass."

Past Perfect Continuous

"Mariella had been studying a lot, and she got a good grade."

5

Future Forms & Structures

Present Continuous / Be going to / Will
Three Primary Future Structures
Present Continuous (Fixed Arrangement)

"Bella is sitting a music exam tonight."

be + going to + verb (Intentions)

"Yasmin is going to study English."

Future Simple (will/won't)

"One day, people will study anywhere."

Advanced Future Aspect Combinations
Future Continuous

"In 2070, we will be studying on Mars."

Future Perfect

"By next year, my class will have graduated."

Future Perfect Continuous

"My teacher will have been teaching for 50 yrs!"

✨ Further Expressions: Present simple, about to, expect to, and modals like could or might.

Writing Task 1 & 2 Strategy
🧠 Stative vs Dynamic Meaning Shifts

Stative verbs describe states (not actions) and normally avoid continuous forms. However, at Band 8.0+, recognizing their deliberate shift into progressive aspect demonstrates sophisticated command:

  • • I consider him a leader (State: my static belief/opinion).
  • • I am considering studying abroad (Action: temporary mental process).
  • • The report appears accurate (State: impression) vs He is appearing in court (Action).
📊 Task 1 Data Tense Rules

High-scoring candidates maintain strict tense discipline based on temporal markers in the graph:

  • • Overview / Diagram Intro: Always Present Simple (e.g., "The chart illustrates the proportion...").
  • • Past Data Points: Past Simple (e.g., "Sales plunged in 2015.").
  • • Ongoing Trend up to Now: Present Perfect (e.g., "Consumption has climbed steadily over the last decade.").
