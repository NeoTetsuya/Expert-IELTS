Rule 1

Clause Basics

A clause is a grammatical unit containing a subject and a verb. It can also include an object or complement.

"The sun rises." (Subject + Intransitive Verb)

"Many people like technology." (Subject + Verb + Object)

💡 Every clause must contain at least one Subject + Verb combination!
Rule 2

Combining Clauses

Sentences can combine a main clause and a subordinate clause using conjunctions. Punctuation rules depend on conjunction type and position.

Coordinating (and, but, so)

"Spelling rules exist, but they are manageable."

Subordinating (while, although, whereas)

"Although skiing is tough, it can be mastered."

💡 Notice: Subordinating conjunction in the middle requires NO comma!
Rule 3

Subordinating 'That' Clauses

That clauses are subordinate clauses that cannot stand alone. They follow reporting verbs (*agree, think, claim, report*) to express opinions and beliefs.

"Experts agree that sugar poses a threat."

"Students thought that the exam was fair."

💡 Essential for academic stance in IELTS Task 2 essays!
⭐ Band 8.0+ Error Prevention Eliminating Comma Splices & Run-On Sentences
Common Examiner Penalty Trap

A comma splice occurs when two independent clauses (full sentences) are joined with only a comma. This single error prevents candidates from achieving Band 8.0 in Grammatical Accuracy:

❌ Faulty Comma Splice

"Tuition fees are escalating rapidly, many students cannot afford university."

Two complete ideas connected by only a comma without a conjunction.

✔ 3 High-Band Repairs
  • • Subordination: "Because tuition fees are escalating, many students..."
  • • Semicolon + Adverb: "...escalating rapidly; consequently, many..."
  • • Participle Clause: "...escalating rapidly, preventing many students from..."

2. Cohesive Pronoun Referencing

Avoid word repetition and build logical cohesion using personal, demonstrative, and relative pronouns.

1. Personal Pronouns

he, she, they, their, our

Used specifically to replace named people or groups of people to maintain smooth sentence flow.

"There are five regional managers in the company and they have a lot of experience."

→ 'they' replaces 'five regional managers'.

2. Singular Nouns & Whole Ideas

it, this, that

Replaces singular objects, abstract phenomena, or an entire preceding sentence concept.

"Social networking is a modern phenomenon. It has become popular with people of all ages."

→ 'It' replaces 'social networking'.

3. Plural Nouns & Concepts

these, those

Replaces plural nouns, people, or longer preceding arguments and complex ideas.

"There are some arguments against climate change, but these have mostly been discredited."

→ 'these' replaces 'arguments against climate change'.

4. Relative Pronoun References

which, who, where

Use which for objects/ideas, who for people, and where for places.

"The internet has become a research tool which most people cannot live without."

→ 'which' connects and refers to 'research tool'.

⭐ Band 8.0+ Cohesion Anaphoric Referencing via Summary Nouns
Coherence & Cohesion Criterion

Instead of repeating ambiguous single pronouns ("it", "this"), Band 8+ candidates pair demonstratives with abstract summary nouns to encapsulate complex previous arguments clearly:

Patterns / Trends

"This alarming disparity between urban and rural schools must be addressed."

Replaces: "This is a problem that must be addressed."

Government Policies

"Such fiscal interventions encourage sustainable private investment."

Replaces: "They encourage sustainable investment."

Ongoing Debates

"This contentious issue has triggered intense global discussion."

Replaces: "It has triggered discussion."
