Both... and

Plural Verb Required

Used to show that two distinct subjects or objects share a similar characteristic or action.

"Both the manager and his assistant enjoy training events."

Both + Noun1 + and + Noun2 + Plural Verb

💡 Pronoun form: "Both of them attended..."

Neither... nor

Singular Verb Required

Used to negate two subjects/objects simultaneously ("not this and not that").

"Neither the manager nor his assistant enjoys training events."

Neither + N1 + nor + N2 + Singular Verb

💡 Pronoun form: "Neither of them attended..."

Either... or

Singular Verb / Option

Presents an exclusive choice or option between two subjects or possibilities.

"Either the manager or his assistant should have attended the event."

Either + N1 + or + N2 + Singular/Modal

⭐ Band 8.5+ Syntax Range Negative Inversion & Correlative Parallelism
Writing Task 2 & Speaking Part 3

Placing negative or limiting adverbs at the beginning of a sentence triggers question-word order (Auxiliary + Subject + Verb), creating maximum emphasis and stylistic sophistication:

Not only... but also (Inversion)

"Not only did carbon emissions climb by 14%, but global deforestation also expanded."

Notice the inverted auxiliary 'did' before the subject 'carbon emissions'.

Seldom / Rarely / Under no circumstances

"Seldom do developing nations possess adequate municipal infrastructure to withstand extreme climate events."

Replaces: "Developing nations seldom possess..."

2. Cleft Sentences (Emphasis & Focus)

Connect known context with new information and place powerful emphasis on key details.

Interactive Cleft Sentence Transformer

Academic Emphasis Technique

STANDARD SENTENCE

1. The 'It' Clause

It + to be + noun...

Places new or emphasized information immediately after the opening It + to be clause.

"It was in France that I learnt how to sail."

→ Focus: 'in France'.

💡 Formula: It + is/was + Noun + that/who + Verb

2. The 'What' Clause

What + verb + to be...

Pushes the emphasized new information to the very end of the sentence following is/was.

"What I need to focus on is my listening skills."

→ Focus: 'my listening skills'.

💡 Formula: What + Subject + Verb + is/was + Noun

3. Question Word Clefts

how / why / where...

Uses question words (*how, why, when, where*) to express wonder, surprise, or strong stance.

"How he managed to get that job, I’ll never know."

→ Emphasizes disbelief at the manner/how.

💡 Adds rhetorical power and academic style!
⭐ Band 8.0+ Rhetorical Focus Advanced Pseudo-Clefts for Essay Theses
Writing Task 2 Topic Sentences

Cleft structures allow you to emphasize the crucial premise of your argument at the beginning of a paragraph:

The reason why... is that

"The primary reason why renewable subsidies are essential is that initial capital expenditures remain prohibitive."

It is only by... that

"It is only through decisive legislative reform that income disparity can be curtailed."

All that is needed is...

"All that is required to stimulate grassroots innovation is targeted grant funding."
